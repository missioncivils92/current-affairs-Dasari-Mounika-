## Part B · Mains question with answer outline

**GS-2 · 15 marks · 250 words:** Should Lok Sabha seat shares stay frozen to protect states that controlled population? Discuss with reference to women's reservation and delimitation.

- **Introduction:**
  - Articles 81 and 82 tie seats to population.
  - The 84th Amendment (2001) froze seat shares until the first census after 2026.
  - Under Article 334A, the 106th Amendment's one-third reservation for women starts only after delimitation.
- **Arguments for continuing the freeze:**
  - It rewards states that controlled population (mostly southern).
  - It protects federal balance.
  - It avoids a large shift of political weight to a few states.
- **Arguments against:**
  - "One person, one vote": voters in fast-growing states are under-represented.
  - Women's reservation stays blocked while delimitation waits.
  - The April 2026 bill (298–230) shows the deadlock.
- **Middle paths:**
  - Enlarge the House (543 → up to 850) so that no state loses seats.
  - Delink women's reservation from delimitation.
  - Strengthen the Rajya Sabha's federal role.
  - Weigh population with other criteria.
- **Conclusion:** representation needs both equality of votes and federal trust. Build consensus through the Inter-State Council or an all-party process before any special session.
