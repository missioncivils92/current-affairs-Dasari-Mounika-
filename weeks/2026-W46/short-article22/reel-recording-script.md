## Script

Police pick you up at 10 in the morning, but write down your arrest only at night. When does your 24-hour clock start?

Supreme Court ఇప్పుడే చెప్పింది!

Let's decode.

Article 22, clause 2, of our Constitution says an arrested person must be produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours, leaving out only the travel time.

In a drug case from Tripura, the police held four people for searches and paperwork, and recorded the arrest much later. On November 5, the Supreme Court said there is no difference between detention and arrest: the clock starts the moment your liberty is curtailed, and it ordered the four released.

It also overruled the Orissa, Kerala and Madras High Courts, which had allowed such time to be left out.

అంటే, informal detention కి ఇక chance లేదు.

Prelims trap one: only the journey to the magistrate is excluded. Trap two: under Article 22, clause 3, these safeguards do not apply to an enemy alien or to a person held under preventive detention.

ఇది గుర్తుపెట్టుకోండి!

Mains question: is the first hour of custody the real test of personal liberty?
