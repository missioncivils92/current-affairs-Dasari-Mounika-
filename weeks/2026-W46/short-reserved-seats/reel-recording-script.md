## Script

Your constituency is reserved for Scheduled Castes today. Will it ever change?

Supreme Court clear గా చెప్పింది!

Let's decode.

Articles 330 and 332 of the Constitution reserve seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the Lok Sabha and in state assemblies.

A petitioner asked the Supreme Court to rotate these reserved seats from election to election. On November 4, a bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant refused, and upheld Section 9(1)(c) of the Delimitation Act of 2002, which places reserved seats where these communities are comparatively large.

Why? The Court said rotation is a policy choice for Parliament, and a court cannot rewrite the Constitution or order Parliament to make a law.

అంటే, ball ఇప్పుడు Parliament court లో ఉంది.

Prelims trap: rotation of reserved seats does exist, but only in panchayats and municipalities, under Articles 243D and 243T, not in the Lok Sabha or the assemblies.

ఇది గుర్తుపెట్టుకోండి!

Mains debate: should reserved seats rotate, so that more areas get a chance, or stay fixed for stable representation?
