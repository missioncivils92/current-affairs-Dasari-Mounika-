## Script

**How it flows:** the strange idea → the vote → how India voted and why → why it matters to us → trap → Mains debate.

**📷 FULL FACE: *intriguing, "strange but true".***

**What if** a law passed in Washington could punish a company in Hyderabad?

🗣️ **వినడానికి వింతగా ఉంది కదా?**

Last week at the United Nations,

🗣️ **ఇదే పెద్ద debate.**

Let's decode.

**🎙️ *clear, news voice.***

Last week, the **United Nations General Assembly** voted, for the **34th time**, to end the American embargo on Cuba, in place since **1962**. It passed with **130 votes**, far fewer than last year's **165**.

**🎙️ *curious, then explaining.***

**And how did India vote?** **In favour, again.** Because India rejects laws with **extraterritorial effects**, where one country's law reaches into other countries. Under America's **Helms-Burton Act of 1996**, even foreign companies dealing in Cuba can be sued.

🗣️ **అంటే, వేరే దేశం చట్టం మన companies మీద కూడా పడొచ్చు.**

**🎙️ *connecting to us, serious.***

**So why does this matter to us?** Because such pressure touches India too: American sanctions on Iran have threatened India's **Chabahar port** project.

🗣️ **అందుకే India strategic autonomy ని కాపాడుకుంటుంది.**

**🎙️ *slower, "write this down".***

**Prelims trap:** a General Assembly resolution is **not binding**; only **Security Council** decisions bind, under **Article 25** of the United Nations Charter. So the embargo stays, but the message is loud.

**📷 FULL FACE: *thoughtful, inviting.***

**Mains debate:** can India stay friends with America and still vote against its embargo?

🗣️ **మీ అభిప్రాయం comments లో రాయండి**, and follow for one exam trap every day!

*(Stay still and smile for 2 seconds.)*

**Pronunciation:** extraterritorial [EX-tra-te-ri-TOR-ee-al] · Helms-Burton [HELMZ BUR-ton] · Chabahar [CHAA-ba-haar]
