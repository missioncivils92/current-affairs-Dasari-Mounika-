**🎙️ NARRATION**

Last week, the United Nations General Assembly voted, for the 34th time, to end the American embargo on Cuba, in place since 1962. It passed with 130 votes, far fewer than last year's 165.

And how did India vote? In favour, again. Because India rejects laws with extraterritorial effects, where one country's law reaches into other countries. Under America's Helms-Burton Act of 1996, even foreign companies dealing in Cuba can be sued. Ante, vere desam chattam mana companies meeda kooda padochu.

So why does this matter to us? Because such pressure touches India too: American sanctions on Iran have threatened India's Chabahar port project. Anduke India strategic autonomy ni kaapaadukuntundi.

**🎙️ NARRATION — *slower.***

Prelims trap: a General Assembly resolution is not binding; only Security Council decisions bind, under Article 25 of the United Nations Charter. So the embargo stays, but the message is loud.

Mains debate: can India stay friends with America and still vote against its embargo? Mee abhiprayam comments lo rayandi, and follow for one exam trap every day!
