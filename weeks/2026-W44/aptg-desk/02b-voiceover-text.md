## AP–TG DESK — AP–TG Desk: seven Telugu States topics, exam format

Save as: `aptg-desk_VO_aptg.wav`

**🎙️ NARRATION — *warm, bright.***

Decode U-P-S-C: unlock your potential, decode your future. This is your Andhra Pradesh and Telangana Desk for week forty-four.

**🎙️ NARRATION**

Seven topics from the Telugu States this week, and for each one, why it is in the news, the static facts, the trap, and a practice question in the Group-1 style.

Topic one: the Backward Classes quota in Andhra Pradesh's local bodies, from Polity.

Why in news: on the first of October, the Andhra Pradesh High Court set aside the orders giving Backward Classes thirty-four per cent of seats in rural local bodies; on the ninth, the Supreme Court dismissed the State's appeal, and the State Cabinet has since adopted its dedicated commission's report.

The rule comes from the Supreme Court's triple test, set in the Gawali case of 2021: a dedicated commission must study backwardness, the quota must be fixed body by body, and total reservation must stay within fifty per cent. It builds on K. Krishna Murthy in 2010, and on Indra Sawhney's fifty per cent ceiling of 1992.

Prelims Trap. Article two-four-three D, clause six, only enables a Backward Classes quota in panchayats; it does not make one compulsory, and the quota must still pass the triple test.

Pause and try this one: how many of these three statements are correct?

The answer is only two: statements two and three, because the Article only enables a quota; it does not require one.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.

Topic two: an acting Chief Justice for the Telangana High Court, from Polity.

Why in news: Justice P. Sam Koshy [Sam KO-shee] is now the acting Chief Justice of the Telangana High Court, after the Chief Justice was elevated to the Supreme Court.

Know the High Court Articles as a set. Article two-fourteen gives each State a High Court; two-seventeen covers how judges are appointed; two-twenty-three, the acting Chief Justice; two-twenty-four, additional and acting judges; two-twenty-four A, retired judges; and two-thirty-one, one High Court for two or more States.

Prelims Trap. An acting Chief Justice is appointed by the President under Article two-twenty-three, not by the Chief Justice of India.

Pause and try this one: which of these statements are correct?

The answer is option b, one and two only, because Article two-twenty-four A is about retired judges sitting in High Courts.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.

Topic three, our Telugu States Lens: who pays Andhra Pradesh's pension, from Social Justice.

Why in news: under Andhra Pradesh's N-T-R Bharosa [Bha-ROH-saa] scheme, the State pays an elderly pensioner four thousand rupees a month, of which the Centre's share is only two hundred.

The Centre's part comes from the National Social Assistance Programme of the Rural Development Ministry: two hundred rupees for the elderly aged sixty to seventy-nine and five hundred from eighty; three hundred for widows and for persons with severe disabilities; a one-time twenty thousand rupees under the family benefit scheme; and ten kilograms of free grain a month under Annapurna.

Prelims Trap. The National Social Assistance Programme is run by the Ministry of Rural Development, not by the Social Justice Ministry.

Pause and try this one: how many of these three statements are correct?

The answer is all three, because each statement matches the programme's rules.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.

Topic four: the Krishna and Godavari river boards, from Geography and Polity.

Why in news: the Krishna and Godavari river management boards were scheduled to meet on the twenty-sixth and the thirtieth of October, with the Polavaram [Poh-laa-va-ram] backwater on the Godavari board's agenda.

Know who settles what. The two boards were created by the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act of 2014, with an Apex Council of the Union Jal Shakti Minister and both Chief Ministers above them, and Polavaram is a national project under the same Act. Water disputes, by contrast, go to tribunals under a 1956 Act made under Article two-sixty-two: the Krishna tribunals of 1973 and 2010, and the Godavari tribunal of 1980.

Prelims Trap. The river boards are not tribunals under Article two-sixty-two; they are bodies created by the 2014 Reorganisation Act.

Pause and try this one: how many of these three statements are correct?

The answer is all three, because the boards and Polavaram's status come from the 2014 Act, and the tribunals from the 1956 Act.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.

Topic five: the Tummidihatti [Tum-mi-di-HUT-ti] barrage, from Geography.

Why in news: on the twenty-sixth of October, Telangana's Chief Minister met Maharashtra's in Mumbai over the Tummidihatti barrage. Telangana wants a full reservoir level of one hundred and fifty-two metres, against the one hundred and forty-eight agreed in 2016, and the two States agreed to a joint committee of officials.

The barrage is on the Pranahita [Praa-na-HEE-ta], which forms where the Wardha meets the Wainganga, runs along the Telangana–Maharashtra border, and joins the Godavari at Kaleshwaram.

Prelims Trap. The barrage is not on the Godavari's main stem; it is on the Pranahita, a tributary.

Pause and try this one: how many of these three statements are correct?

The answer is only one: statement one, because the Pranahita joins the Godavari, and the barrage is on the Pranahita, not on the main stem.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.

Topic six: who runs a disaster response, from Disaster Management.

Why in news: as Cyclone Arnab came ashore on the Godavari delta coast, Andhra Pradesh's response ran through its State Disaster Management Authority.

Under the Disaster Management Act of 2005, the national authority is chaired by the Prime Minister, the State authority by the Chief Minister, and the district authority by the Collector; and the National Disaster Response Force is set up under section forty-four of the same Act.

Prelims Trap. The State authority is chaired by the Chief Minister, not by the Governor.

Pause and try this one: which of these statements are correct?

The answer is option b, one and three only, because the State authority is chaired by the Chief Minister.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.

Topic seven: Kolleru [Kol-LAY-ru] Lake, from Environment.

Why in news: Andhra Pradesh has asked the Supreme Court's Central Empowered Committee to exclude about twenty thousand acres from the Kolleru Wildlife Sanctuary, as reported.

Know the lakes that UPSC sets side by side. Kolleru is a freshwater lake between the Krishna and Godavari deltas, a Ramsar site since 2002; Chilika and Pulicat are brackish lagoons; Sambhar is saline; and Loktak is freshwater, with floating phumdis.

Prelims Trap. Kolleru is not a brackish lagoon like Chilika; it is a freshwater lake.

Pause and try this one: how many of these three statements are correct?

The answer is only two: statements two and three, because Kolleru is freshwater, not brackish.

Write this down, and pause if you need a moment.
