## 3. Five-step analysis (summary; the full narration is in the script)

**Step 1: Trigger.**
- 5 Oct: notice issued; interim suspension refused; the CJI said the Court cannot act on newspaper reports alone, and that no modified Form 6 has been approved by the Court.
- 26 Oct: the ECI affidavit said the three SIR orders were unanimous; individual members' notes are not decisions; Form 6 is unchanged; the SIR declaration is a separate document.
- 27 Oct: adjourned; next hearing 17 Nov.

**Step 2: Foundations.**
- Art 324(1) and (2); Art 324(5) and its proviso.
- CEC & ECs Act 2023: s.18 (unanimity as far as possible; otherwise the majority prevails); s.16 (immunity, cited by the petitioners).
- RP Act 1950 s.28 → Registration of Electors Rules 1960 (Central Government, after consulting the ECI) → Form 6.
- The ERO prepares the roll.

**Step 3: The chain.**
- The body became plural by statute and was upheld in *Seshan*. **Therefore** decisions are collegial (s.18).
- **But** the Constitution's removal shield covers only the CEC (Art 324(5)). **Therefore** "equal votes, unequal security" is the structural question behind the petitions.
- **Separately,** forms belong to the Centre's rules, so whether the SIR declaration "amends" Form 6 is a rule-making question.

**Step 4a: Traps** (with the whole set).
1. EC removal is on the CEC's recommendation; only the CEC has SC-judge removal.
2. The number of ECs is fixed by the President (Art 324(2)); the Constitution fixes no number.
3. The registration rules are made by the Central Government, not the ECI; the roll is prepared by the ERO. Also not the DEO, and not the State Election Commission, which runs local-body polls only.
4. Under s.18, when the members differ the majority decides, not the CEC (on screen as the Statement-I/II MCQ).

**Step 4b: Mains Lens.**

| Dimension | Points with data |
|---|---|
| Independence (Governance) | 2023 panel: PM + a Union Cabinet Minister + LoP; *Anoop Baranwal* (2023) had included the CJI; split order of 23 Sep 2026, placed before the CJI for a larger bench |
| Federal balance | Rolls prepared by State government officers (EROs) under ECI superintendence |
| Rights (Socio-political) | Art 326 adult suffrage; the SC said SIR complaints "cannot be generalised" and will be examined State by State (26 Oct, Maharashtra plea, which alleges deletions; these are allegations, not findings) |
| National security | Not forced. Electoral-roll integrity is a sovereignty issue only in the abstract, so it is left out rather than padded |

The contestable proposition (FOR / AGAINST) is the `[EXT]` row 2.12.

**Step 5: Way forward.**
- Law Commission **255th Report (March 2015)**: amend Art 324(5) to equate the ECs' removal with the CEC's. The Goswami Committee (1990) made a similar point; that is reported, not checked.
- Record dissents and decisions under s.18 (framed as an option).
- India's imperative: trust in the referee, built on structure.

**Model question:** "Is the Election Commission of India a collegial body in law but a single-member body in practice? Examine." (15 marks, GS-2)
- **Intro:** Art 324 + 1993.
- **Body:** decision rule · security of tenure · rule-making · appointment.
- **Conclusion:** Law Commission 2015 + recorded decisions.

## 4. MCQs (for the PDF; the first is on screen in row 2.9)

1. **Statement-I/II** (on screen). **Answer: (c).** Statement-I is right under Art 324(5); Statement-II is wrong because s.18 gives the decision to the majority.
2. **How many:**
   1. The Constitution fixes the number of ECs at two.
   2. An EC can be removed only on the CEC's recommendation.
   3. The Registration of Electors Rules 1960 are made by the ECI.

   **Answer: only one (statement 2).**
3. **Pairs:** *T.N. Seshan v UoI* : CEC first among equals ✓ · *Anoop Baranwal v UoI* : CJI on the selection panel until Parliament legislates ✓ · RP Act 1950 s.28 : power to make registration rules ✓. **All three pairs are correct.**
