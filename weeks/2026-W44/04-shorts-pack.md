## Short #2 (Tue): Prelims Trap, with a quiz sticker
- **Title:** Is Yanam Part of Andhra Pradesh? #UPSC #Shorts
- **Related video:** Prelims Grid W44, chapter 01:14.
- **On-screen hook text:** Yanam: which State?
- **Quiz sticker:** "Yanam belongs to… Andhra Pradesh / Puducherry / Odisha"
- **Script:**
> Quick question: Yanam sits on the Godavari delta, completely surrounded by Andhra Pradesh, so is it part of Andhra Pradesh? It is not. Yanam belongs to the Union Territory of Puducherry, together with Puducherry itself, Karaikal and Mahe, four pieces of one territory spread across three States' coastlines. This week it was in the news because Cyclone Arnab crossed the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coast, and that is exactly the kind of line the examiner turns into a trap. So the rule is simple: when a place is surrounded by a State, check who actually governs it. The full story, with how the cyclone formed and how it got its name, is in Sunday's Prelims Grid, linked right here.

## Short #3 (Wed): "Write this down": who names a cyclone
- **Title:** Who Names Cyclones Like Arnab? #UPSC #Shorts
- **Related video:** Prelims Grid W44, chapter 01:14.
- **On-screen hook text:** 169 names, 13 countries
- **Script:**
> Who gave Cyclone Arnab its name? Not the I-M-D, and not India. Thirteen countries on the W-M-O and ESCAP panel each gave thirteen names, a list of one hundred and sixty-nine adopted in 2020, and they are used in order. The I-M-D's centre in New Delhi assigns the next name only when a storm reaches cyclonic-storm strength, sixty-two kilometres an hour, and once used, a name is never used again. Arnab was Bangladesh's name. Write this down: thirteen countries, one hundred and sixty-nine names, sixty-two kilometres an hour. The full topic, with how a cyclone forms, is in Sunday's Prelims Grid, linked right here.

## Short #4 (Thu): Telugu States Lens (APPSC-tagged)
- **Title:** Kolleru: Freshwater or Brackish? #APPSC #Shorts
- **Related video:** AP–TG Desk W44, chapter 12:54.
- **On-screen hook text:** Kolleru, Chilika, Pulicat: which is fresh?
- **Script:**
> Three famous lakes, one question: which of these is freshwater? Kolleru, Chilika or Pulicat? The answer is Kolleru. It lies between the Krishna and Godavari deltas in Andhra Pradesh, and it has been a Ramsar site since 2002. Chilika and Pulicat are brackish lagoons, because they open to the sea. This week Kolleru was in the news because Andhra Pradesh has asked the Supreme Court's Central Empowered Committee to exclude about twenty thousand acres from the sanctuary, as reported. And Sunday's challenge answer: only one statement was correct, because the river board is not a tribunal and Pulicat is not freshwater. The full Telugu States desk is linked right here.

## Short #5 (Fri): the examiner's trick
- **Title:** The Ministry Swap: UPSC's Favourite Trap #UPSC #Shorts
- **Related video:** Prelims Grid W44, chapter 28:16.
- **On-screen hook text:** Right fact, wrong ministry
- **Script:**
> Here is the trick UPSC used again and again this week: keep the fact, swap the body. The statement will say DIPAM sits in the Commerce Ministry; it sits in Finance. It will say the Central Pollution Control Board invokes GRAP; it is the Commission for Air Quality Management. It will say the World Economic Forum publishes the Global Innovation Index; it is W-I-P-O. And it will say U-N-E-P's Emissions Gap Report belongs to the I-P-C-C. So whenever a statement names a ministry or a body, stop and ask: is that really the one in charge? That one habit protects you from a whole family of traps. All three of this week's tricks are in Sunday's Prelims Grid, linked right here.
