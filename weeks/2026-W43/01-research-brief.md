## 3. Five-step analysis

### P1 — The El Niño Drought

**Step 1: Trigger**
- The southwest monsoon withdrew from the entire country on 15 Oct and ended about 13% below the long-period average (LPA). (DTE; IMD press release listing) [VERIFY final IMD %]
- Andhra Pradesh declared **388 mandals in 21 of 28 districts** drought-affected for Kharif 2026 (283 severe, 105 moderate). The Revenue (Disaster Management) Department issued the GO around 1–2 Oct. (South Matters; Vajiram & Ravi)
- AP's June–August rain was about 53.4% below normal (197.6 mm against a normal of 424.3 mm). Telangana ended the season about 17% below normal and is assessing mandals; no formal declaration was found.
- IIT Gandhinagar's India Drought Monitor shows about **70.6%** of India's area in dry or drought conditions (DTE; single source). [VERIFY]
- For context, 1987 and 2002 both ended about 19% below normal. Marathwada is about 40% below normal.

**Step 2: Static anchor**
- GS-1 (climatology: monsoon mechanism, ENSO, IOD) and GS-3 (disaster management; agriculture and irrigation).
- **El Niño:** abnormal warming of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific. It weakens the Walker circulation and is linked to weaker Indian monsoons. A positive **Indian Ocean Dipole** can offset it, as happened in 1997 and 2019.
- **IMD all-India seasonal categories:** deficient < 90% of LPA; below normal 90–95%; normal 96–104%; above normal 105–110%; excess > 110%. The LPA for 1971–2020 is about 87 cm. A 13% deficit (about 87% of LPA) therefore falls in the **deficient** category.
- **Who declares drought:** the **state government**, using the *Manual for Drought Management* (2016, updated 2020). The manual's indicators run in a sequence: (i) mandatory rainfall indicators (deviation, SPI, dry spells), then (ii) impact indicators (crop sown area, NDVI/VCI vegetation indices, soil moisture, hydrology). This is followed by a ground-truth survey.
- **Disaster Management Act 2005.** Drought is on the list of notified calamities that can be funded through the **SDRF**, with **NDRF** support when severe. The **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare** is the nodal ministry for drought, not the MHA.
- ***Swaraj Abhiyan v. Union of India* (2016):** the SC directed the Centre to update the drought manual, set up a National Disaster Mitigation Fund, and stop states from under-reporting drought.

**Step 3: Prelims Trap Vault**
1. UPSC may ask "IMD declares drought in a state." **Trap:** IMD only classifies rainfall. **State governments** declare drought, using the Agriculture Ministry's manual.
2. "The nodal ministry for all disasters is the MHA." **Trap:** for **drought**, the nodal ministry is **Agriculture & Farmers Welfare**.
3. "El Niño always means a deficient Indian monsoon." **Trap:** no. A positive IOD can neutralise it (1997 is the textbook case, with near-normal rain in a strong El Niño year).
4. "The India Drought Monitor is run by IMD." **Trap:** it is run by **IIT Gandhinagar**. IMD publishes the SPI and rainfall statistics.
5. "Normal rainfall is 100% of LPA exactly." **Trap:** the "normal" band is **96–104%**.

**Step 4: Mains matrix**
| Dimension | Points |
|---|---|
| Economic & Fiscal | Kharif output risk (pulses, oilseeds, groundnut in Rayalaseema). Food inflation passes through to CPI (Sept food CPI 7.74%; see P2). Rural demand is squeezed. States face SDRF strain plus demands for input subsidies and crop-loss relief |
| Governance & Federal | The declaration is a state decision, but relief funding depends on central norms (SDRF/NDRF), so under-declaration and over-declaration incentives both exist (*Swaraj Abhiyan*). Inter-ministerial central teams assess the damage. Watch delays in MGNREGA wage payment and demand for extra workdays in drought-declared areas [VERIFY current norm] |
| Security & Geopolitics | Food-security buffers (FCI stocks) and possible export curbs on rice and onion affect India's image as a reliable food exporter. Water stress feeds inter-state river disputes (Krishna) |
| Socio-Environmental | Distress migration, farmer suicides, groundwater over-extraction, fodder shortage, women's water-fetching burden. The paradox in DTE's framing: decades of watershed programmes and the conservation measures still could not absorb a 13% deficit |

**Step 5: Way forward**
- **Mihir Shah Committee (2016):** restructure the CWC and CGWB into a single **National Water Commission** and shift from big-dam supply to demand management and aquifer mapping.
- **Swaraj Abhiyan (2016) directions:** an updated manual (done in 2020), a mitigation fund, and early, honest declarations.
- **PMKSY "Per Drop More Crop":** micro-irrigation in drought-prone blocks. Crop diversification away from water-intensive paddy in rain-shadow zones.
- **NITI Aayog Composite Water Management Index:** use it as a state-ranking incentive.
- Move from crisis management to **risk management**, as the Sendai Framework and the NDMA drought guidelines both urge.

**Model Mains question (GS-3, 15 marks):** "A 'moderate' national rainfall deficit can conceal a severe regional drought. Examine with reference to the 2026 monsoon, and suggest how India's drought governance can shift from relief to resilience."
**Skeleton:** Intro: the 13% deficit vs. 70.6% of area dry; AP's 388 mandals. Body: why averages mislead (spatial and temporal distribution, dry spells, groundwater); institutional gaps (declaration incentives, SDRF norms); resilience measures (Mihir Shah, PMKSY, crop diversification, the manual's impact indicators). Conclusion: Sendai Framework, risk-based financing.

**Telugu States Lens:** AP's 388 mandals across 21 districts, 283 of them severe. Rayalaseema (Anantapur, now Anantapur and Sri Sathya Sai districts) is historically the most drought-prone region. AP's assessment went beyond rainfall to include dry spells, reservoir levels, groundwater, soil moisture, sown area and vegetation. Telangana has a 17% deficit and assessments are under way; Mission Kakatiya (tank restoration) is the relevant state programme. Relevant for: APPSC Group-1 Mains (Economy & Development of India and AP; Geography of AP in the History & Culture / Geography paper) and TGPSC Group-1 Mains Paper IV (Economy & Development) / Paper III. ### P2 — The Rate Hike

**Step 1: Trigger**
- The MPC met 5–7 Oct under Governor Sanjay Malhotra and raised the **repo rate by 25 bps, from 5.25% to 5.50%**. The vote was **6–0**. The stance moved from neutral to **"calibrated tightening" on a 4–2 vote**; the dissenters were reportedly Dr Nagesh Kumar and Prof Ram Singh. **SDF 5.25%, MSF and Bank Rate 5.75%.** This is the first hike since Feb 2023. Minutes were published 21 Oct. (SCC Online; PSU Connect; CorpLawUpdates) [VERIFY vote and stance wording on rbi.org.in]
- **September CPI: 5.54%** (August 4.83%). This is the provisional figure on the **new CPI series, base year 2024**, released 12 Oct. Food inflation (CFPI) was **7.74%**; rural 6.08%, urban 4.88%; onion about +108% year on year. (MoSPI press release; JM Financial)
- The RBI's FY27 CPI projection was raised to 5.2%. A GDP projection of about 7.1% has been reported. [VERIFY both against the October MPR]

**Step 2: Static anchor**
- GS-3: monetary policy, inflation, growth.
- **RBI Act 1934, as amended in 2016:**
  - **s.45ZA:** the **Central Government**, in consultation with the RBI, sets the inflation target once every five years. The current framework is CPI 4% ± 2%. [VERIFY whether the target was retained at the March 2026 review]
  - **s.45ZB:** the **MPC has 6 members**: 3 from the RBI (Governor as chair, Deputy Governor, one officer) and 3 external members appointed by the Centre. The quorum is 4. The Governor has a casting vote on a tie.
  - **s.45ZN:** "failure" means average inflation outside the band for **three consecutive quarters**. The RBI must then send the government a report on the causes and remedial action.
- **Urjit Patel Committee (2014)** recommended flexible inflation targeting (FIT) on CPI.
- **LAF corridor:** SDF (floor) = repo − 25 bps; MSF (ceiling) = repo + 25 bps.

**Step 3: Prelims Trap Vault**
1. "The RBI sets India's inflation target." **Trap:** the **Central Government** sets it, in consultation with the RBI (s.45ZA).
2. "The MPC decides the CRR and SLR." **Trap:** the MPC fixes **only the policy repo rate**. CRR is set by the RBI outside the MPC; SLR is set by the RBI under the Banking Regulation Act.
3. "All six MPC members are RBI officials." **Trap:** **three are external**, appointed by the Centre.
4. "The floor of the LAF corridor is the reverse repo." **Trap:** since 2022 the floor has been the **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)**, which needs no collateral.
5. "CPI is compiled by the RBI / Labour Bureau." **Trap:** CPI (Rural, Urban, Combined) is compiled by the **NSO under MoSPI**. CPI-IW is from the Labour Bureau.

**Step 4: Mains matrix**
| Dimension | Points |
|---|---|
| Economic & Fiscal | **Supply-shock dilemma:** the rate tool works through demand, but food inflation is driven by supply (drought, onion). The hike aims to anchor **expectations** and prevent second-round effects (wages, core inflation). Costs: higher EMIs, MSME credit costs, a growth trade-off |
| Governance & Federal | MPC independence and the 4–2 split on stance show healthy debate. Fiscal–monetary coordination: supply-side tools (buffer stocks, export curbs, stock limits) sit with the government, not the RBI |
| Security & Geopolitics | Crude oil price pass-through. Rate differentials affect capital flows and the rupee. Imported inflation |
| Socio-Environmental | Inflation is a regressive tax: rural inflation (6.08%) exceeds urban (4.88%), so climate shocks become a cost-of-living crisis for the poor |

**Step 5: Way forward**
- Strengthen supply-side buffers: the **Price Stabilisation Fund**, onion buffer stocks, **Operation Greens (TOP: tomato, onion, potato)** and better cold storage.
- Keep FIT credible. The Urjit Patel framework's strength is expectation anchoring, so a premature reversal would hurt.
- Improve data: the new CPI base (2024) has updated weights, so communicate them clearly to avoid misreading.
- Align with climate adaptation: drought-proof agriculture (P1) is also anti-inflation policy.

**Model Mains question (GS-3, 10 marks):** "Can monetary tightening control inflation that is driven by supply shocks? Discuss in the context of the RBI's October 2026 policy."
**Skeleton:** Intro: the hike and the CPI print. Body: limits (food supply) vs. rationale (expectations, second-round effects, core inflation); complementary fiscal and administrative tools. Conclusion: coordinated policy.

**Telugu lens (light):** Kurnool is AP's main onion belt. Drought-hit rural districts see higher rural CPI. Include one line only.

### P3 — Internet as a Fundamental Right, for children too

**Step 1: Trigger**
- *Just Rights for Children Alliance & Anr. v. S. Harish & Ors.* — Justices **J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran**. Order dated **15 Oct 2026**, reported widely from about 17 Oct. It arose from applications to implement the Court's **23 Sep 2024** judgment on CSAM. (LiveLaw; Bar & Bench; Onmanorama)
- Holding: the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right of **every individual, including children**, under **Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a)**.
- The Court rejected **blanket age-based bans** and favoured proportionate safeguards: parental consent and oversight, a "child mode", private-by-default accounts, limits on contact from unknown adults, and moderation of ads for pre-teens. Modalities are left to the Union. The State may still block inherently harmful categories such as adult sites.
- The Court flagged privacy risks in age-verification systems that collect sensitive data.
- **CSAM directions:** a **National Hash and URL List Database**; entries require assessment by at least **three trained human analysts**; intermediaries must remove matches immediately and **report removals of material of unknown origin within two hours**; grievance officers and monthly compliance reports; the Union is to consider exemplary fines and **disgorgement** of revenue.
- **Caveat:** one report says the Court did not opine on whether internet access is *by itself* a fundamental right. Most reports quote the broader holding. [VERIFY exact wording in the 137-page order]

**Step 2: Static anchor**
- GS-2: Fundamental Rights, judiciary, government policy (IT governance), vulnerable sections (children). GS-4: autonomy vs. protection.
- ***Anuradha Bhasin v. UoI* (2020):** freedom of speech and trade **through the medium of the internet** is protected under Art 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). Shutdowns must meet necessity and proportionality, and orders must be published. It did **not** declare internet access itself a fundamental right.
- ***Faheema Shirin v. State of Kerala* (Kerala HC, 2019):** the right to internet access is part of the right to education and privacy under Art 21.
- ***K.S. Puttaswamy* (2017):** privacy is a fundamental right, with the proportionality test.
- **2024 judgment (same case title):** viewing, possessing or storing CSAM can attract **POCSO s.15** and **IT Act s.67B**. The Court urged Parliament to replace "child pornography" with **"Child Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Material" (CSEAM)**.
- **DPDP Act 2023, s.9:** verifiable parental consent is needed to process a child's data (child = under 18); tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads at children are barred. (Link to the Oct 2026 order is our analysis; the reports don't cite it.)
- **Art 21A:** free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14.

**Step 3: Prelims Trap Vault**
1. "*Anuradha Bhasin* declared internet access a fundamental right." **Trap:** it protected speech and trade **through** the internet under Art 19. The Oct 2026 order is the one that speaks of the right to access and use the internet, grounded in **Art 21 read with 19(1)(a)**.
2. "A fundamental right to the internet obliges the State to give free internet to all." **Trap:** the order bars **arbitrary or blanket denial** and requires proportionality. Reports do not show it creating an entitlement to free connectivity.
3. "Under the DPDP Act a child is anyone below 16." **Trap:** the DPDP Act defines a child as **below 18**.
4. "POCSO is administered by the MeitY." **Trap:** the nodal ministry for POCSO is the **Ministry of Women and Child Development**. The IT Act is MeitY's.
5. "The first state to declare internet a basic right was Karnataka." **Trap:** **Kerala** (2017 policy announcement; K-FON).

**Step 4: Mains matrix**
| Dimension | Points |
|---|---|
| Economic & Fiscal | The digital economy depends on access (education, DBT, UPI). Platform compliance costs: hash-matching, 2-hour reporting. Disgorgement as a deterrent |
| Governance & Federal | Judicial direction vs. legislative domain (separation of powers). The Union must design the modalities. Interplay with the IT Rules 2021 and the DPDP Rules [VERIFY status]. State-ordered shutdowns now face a higher bar |
| Security & Geopolitics | CSAM is cross-border: hash databases align with global practice (e.g., NCMEC/IWF-style systems). Encryption vs. detection debates |
| Socio-Environmental | The digital divide (gender, rural). Children's development, mental health, the online-harm evidence base. Rejecting age bans contrasts with blanket under-16 social-media bans abroad, such as Australia's 2024 law [VERIFY in force date] |

**Step 5: Way forward**
- A legislative framework for children's online safety (safety-by-design codes, age-appropriate design) instead of blanket bans, in line with the order's proportionality approach.
- Amend POCSO terminology to "CSEAM", as the 2024 judgment recommended.
- **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Communications & IT:** reports on cyber-crime and data protection [cite exact report when finalising, or drop]. Privacy-preserving age assurance.
- Bridge the access gap: BharatNet, PM-WANI. Digital literacy (PMGDISHA).

**Model Mains question (GS-2, 15 marks):** "The Supreme Court has recognised the right to access and use the internet as a fundamental right, including for children, while rejecting blanket age-based bans. Critically examine how the State can balance child safety with digital freedom."
**Skeleton:** Intro: the holding plus the Bhasin lineage. Body: rights basis (21, 19(1)(a), 21A); risks (CSAM, addiction, data); proportional tools (consent, child mode, hash DB, DPDP); separation-of-powers concerns. Conclusion: safety-by-design over prohibition.

**GS-4 hook:** paternalism vs. autonomy. "Protection that removes the right to explore is not protection, it is confinement."

### P4 — India–New Zealand FTA: trade without dairy

**Step 1: Trigger**
- The India–NZ FTA **entered into force on 20 Oct 2026**. It was signed **27 Apr 2026** in New Delhi; New Zealand passed implementing legislation on 16 Sep 2026. (Newkerala; CivilsDaily; Annapurna Express)
- **India's offer:** concessions on about **70% (70.03%) of tariff lines**. **New Zealand's offer:** Indian exports reportedly get 100% duty-free access. NZ exports get duty-free or reduced entry on about 95% of lines (sources word this differently). [VERIFY via Commerce Ministry PIB release]
- **Dairy excluded:** milk, cream, whey, yogurt and cheese. Some dairy-based preparations (e.g., bulk infant formula) are phased out over 7 years, with a consultation clause. Onions, chickpeas, peas, almonds, sugar and artificial honey are also excluded. **Apples, kiwifruit and manuka honey** come in under **seasonal tariff-rate quotas with minimum import prices**.
- New Zealand has committed to facilitating about **USD 20 billion** of investment.

**Step 2: Static anchor**
- GS-2: bilateral and regional groupings; India's Indo-Pacific policy. GS-3: trade, agriculture (dairy).
- **GATT Article XXIV** allows FTAs and customs unions as an exception to WTO MFN treatment, if they cover "substantially all trade".
- **India walked out of RCEP in Nov 2019**, citing dairy and agriculture import surges and concern about China-routed goods. New Zealand, a dairy superpower, is an RCEP member.
- India's recent FTA arc: **India–Australia ECTA** (in force Dec 2022); **India–EFTA TEPA** (in force 1 Oct 2025, with a USD 100 bn investment commitment; 2nd Prosperity Summit 7 Oct 2026); **India–UK CETA** (signed July 2025); India–Canada CEPA talks (5th round from 5 Oct 2026).
- Dairy context: India is the world's largest milk producer. Dairy is a livelihood for smallholders and a heavily cooperative sector (Amul/NDDB).

**Step 3: Prelims Trap Vault**
1. "Trade agreements are negotiated by the MEA." **Trap:** the **Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce & Industry**.
2. "EFTA is part of the European Union." **Trap:** EFTA = **Iceland, Liechtenstein [LIK-ten-shtyne], Norway, Switzerland**, none of them EU members.
3. "India is a member of RCEP." **Trap:** India **withdrew in 2019**. New Zealand and Australia are members.
4. "A tariff-rate quota bans imports above the quota." **Trap:** a TRQ applies a **lower tariff within the quota** and a higher tariff above it. It is not a ban.
5. "FTAs violate WTO rules." **Trap:** they are permitted under **GATT Art XXIV** (goods) and **GATS Art V** (services).

**Step 4: Mains matrix**
| Dimension | Points |
|---|---|
| Economic & Fiscal | Market access for labour-intensive Indian exports (textiles, pharma, engineering). Investment facilitation (USD 20 bn). Tariff revenue loss is small given the limited bilateral trade base. TRQs with minimum import prices protect apple growers (J&K, Himachal) |
| Governance & Federal | Agriculture is a State subject, but trade is Union: the federal consultation question. Parliament has no ratification role for trade treaties in India (executive power, Art 73). Is there a case for parliamentary scrutiny? |
| Security & Geopolitics | Indo-Pacific: NZ is a Five Eyes member and Pacific partner. Diversification away from China-centred supply chains. Signals India is an FTA-ready partner after RCEP |
| Socio-Environmental | Dairy exclusion protects about 8 crore dairy households [VERIFY figure] and women's livelihoods. Mobility provisions (students, professionals). Sustainable-trade chapters |

**Step 5: Way forward**
- A template of "**calibrated openness**": red lines (dairy, sensitive agriculture) plus TRQs and minimum import prices instead of all-or-nothing.
- Use FTA utilisation data. Indian exporters historically under-use FTAs (low utilisation rates flagged in NITI Aayog and Commerce Ministry studies [VERIFY citation]), so MSMEs need rules-of-origin facilitation.
- **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Commerce:** recommendations on stakeholder consultation before FTAs [cite report or drop].
- Leverage the NZ deal for the Pacific Islands outreach (FIPIC).

**Model Mains question (GS-2, 10 marks):** "India's FTA with New Zealand shows a shift from defensive to calibrated trade engagement. Discuss with reference to India's withdrawal from RCEP."
**Skeleton:** Intro: in force 20 Oct; dairy red line. Body: RCEP concerns, how the NZ deal answers them (exclusions, TRQs, MIPs), benefits, risks. Conclusion: a template for the EU and Canada talks.

**RP Act s.125A ruling (16 Oct):** *Rajkumar Madki v. Dr. Shailendra Beldale*, Justices Dipankar Datta & Sheel Nagu. Private individuals can file criminal complaints for false or concealed information in nomination affidavits. The procedural bar requiring a complaint from a public servant (s.195 CrPC; now BNSS) does not apply. Magistrates can dismiss frivolous complaints. The Court upheld the quashing of the specific complaint against the Karnataka MLA on other grounds. **Penalty under s.125A:** imprisonment up to 6 months, or fine, or both. (LiveLaw; Telangana Today; Drishti Judiciary)
