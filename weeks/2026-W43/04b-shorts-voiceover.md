**Voice:** curious and quick on the opening question; clear and steady on the facts; crisp on the trap.

Russia and Ukraine — why is there still no peace? The full-scale war began on the twenty-fourth of February, 2022. But the roots go back to 2014 — when Russia annexed Crimea [Kry-MEE-ya], and fighting began in the Donbas. Russia cites NATO's eastward expansion. Ukraine calls it an invasion of a sovereign state.

The latest? There is still no full ceasefire. From the twenty-seventh of October, a local truce begins in Oleshky [Oh-LESH-kee], in the Kherson [Kher-SON] region — only to let aid in. New US-mediated talks are planned for November, in Abu Dhabi. The sticking points — territory in the Donbas, and security guarantees.

**Prelims Trap** — Ukraine is not a NATO member. And India? It has abstained on UN votes, and calls for dialogue and diplomacy.

**Save as:** `2026-W43_SHORT1_rp_act_125a.wav`
**Length:** 110 words · about 49 seconds

Can an ordinary voter take a lying candidate to court? This month, the Supreme Court said yes. On the sixteenth of October, a bench of Justices Dipankar Datta [Dee-pun-kar DUT-ta] and Sheel Nagu [Sheel NAA-goo] ruled on false election affidavits. A voter can now file a private criminal complaint against a candidate who hides or falsifies information. You do not have to wait for an official or the Election Commission. The provision — Section one-twenty-five A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951. **Prelims Trap** — the penalty is up to six months in jail, a fine, or both.
