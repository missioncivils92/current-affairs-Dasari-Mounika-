## Short #1 — False election affidavits: any citizen can complain
- **Title (≤50 chars):** Can a voter take a liar to court? #UPSC #Shorts
- **On-screen hook text:** "Lied in the affidavit?"
- **Status:** verified (LiveLaw; Telangana Today). **Publish Mon 2 Nov**, after Sunday's video reveals the MCQ challenge answer. Set Related video to the W43 masterclass.
- **Script (~115 words):**
> Can an ordinary voter take a lying candidate to court? This month, the Supreme Court said yes. On the sixteenth of October, a bench of Justices Dipankar Datta [Dee-pun-kar DUT-ta] and Sheel Nagu [Sheel NAA-goo] ruled on false election affidavits. A voter can now file a private criminal complaint against a candidate who hides or falsifies information. You do not have to wait for an official or the Election Commission. The provision — Section one-twenty-five A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951. **Prelims Trap** — the penalty is up to six months in jail, a fine, or both. And magistrates can still reject frivolous complaints. Full breakdown in the video linked below.
