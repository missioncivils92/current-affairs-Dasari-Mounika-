One town gets a **truce**. The war does not.

From the twenty-seventh of October, Russia and Ukraine say they will hold fire around Oleshky [Oh-LESH-kee] — across the Dnipro [DNEE-pro] river from Kherson [Kher-SON]. The aim — clear mines, bring in aid, and let civilians leave. It is local. It is temporary. And it shows how far peace still is.

First — why this war? The roots go back to 2014. Russia annexed Crimea [Kry-MEE-ya], and fighting began in the Donbas. Then, on the twenty-fourth of February, 2022, came the full-scale invasion. Russia cites NATO's eastward expansion. Ukraine calls it an attack on a sovereign state.

Now — the numbers. The war has passed seventeen hundred days. Russia controls about nineteen per cent of Ukraine, Crimea included, says the DeepState map.

The human cost keeps rising. In September alone, the UN verified three hundred and twenty-eight civilians killed. From January to September — over two and a half thousand killed. Total civilian casualties are fifty-nine per cent higher than the same months last year. Nearly five point eight million Ukrainians live as refugees. Another three point eight million are displaced inside the country.

And there is a nuclear risk. Zaporizhzhia [Za-po-RIZH-zhya] — Europe's largest nuclear plant — lost all outside power in August. An IAEA-brokered local truce in September allowed repairs.

So where is diplomacy? Talks are expected in mid-November, possibly in Abu Dhabi. No date is fixed. Moscow reportedly wants the rest of the Donbas. Kyiv says territory is a red line — and rejects any cap on its army.

**Prelims Trap.** Donbas means Donetsk [Da-NYETSK] and Luhansk [Loo-HAANSK] — not Crimea. And Zaporizhzhia sits on the Dnipro, not the Don.

Finally — India. India has abstained on UN votes and calls for dialogue and diplomacy. Prime Minister Modi's message — this is not an era of war. And Russia remains India's **largest** crude supplier. In September — about one point eight million barrels a day. That is roughly a third of India's oil imports.

For Mains, remember one line... India's strategic autonomy is being tested — between energy security and a rules-based order.
