Russia and Ukraine — why is there still no peace?

The full-scale war began on the twenty-fourth of February, 2022. But the roots go back to 2014 — when Russia annexed Crimea [Kry-MEE-ya], and fighting began in the Donbas. Russia cites NATO's eastward expansion. Ukraine calls it an invasion of a sovereign state.

The latest? There is still no full ceasefire. From the twenty-seventh of October, a local truce begins in Oleshky [Oh-LESH-kee], in the Kherson [Kher-SON] region — only to let aid in. New US-mediated talks are planned for November, in Abu Dhabi.

The sticking points... Russia wants the whole Donbas. Ukraine refuses to give up land it controls — and wants security guarantees.

And India? It has abstained on UN votes, and calls for dialogue and diplomacy.
