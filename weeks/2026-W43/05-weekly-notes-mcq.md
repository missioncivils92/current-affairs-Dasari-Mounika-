## Pillar 1 — The El Niño Drought `GS-1 · GS-3 · Group-1`

**Why in news**

- The southwest monsoon **withdrew from the entire country on 15 Oct 2026**. It ended about **13% below the long period average (LPA)**, roughly 87% of LPA.
- **Andhra Pradesh** declared **388 mandals in 21 of its 28 districts** drought-hit for Kharif 2026: **283 severe, 105 moderate** (order dated 1 Oct 2026).
- AP's June–August rainfall was about **53% below normal**.
- **Marathwada** ended the season more than a third below normal.
- **IIT Gandhinagar's India Drought Monitor**, as reported by Down To Earth, at one point showed over **70% of India's land** as dry or in drought.
- For comparison, **1987 and 2002**, two of the worst drought years, each ended about **19%** short.

**Static anchor**

- **El Niño:** abnormal warming of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific. It weakens the Walker circulation and is linked to a weaker Indian monsoon.
- **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD):** a *positive* IOD (warmer western Indian Ocean) can offset El Niño. *Example:* 1997 was a strong El Niño year, yet rainfall was near-normal.
- **IMD all-India seasonal categories (% of LPA):**

| Deficient | Below normal | Normal | Above normal | Excess |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 90 | 90–95 | 96–104 | 105–110 | > 110 |

- **Sub-division scale is different:** for a single meteorological sub-division, "normal" is −19% to +19%. Telangana's −17% therefore still counts as *normal*.
- **Who declares drought:** the **state government**, following the *Manual for Drought Management* (first issued 2009, revised 2016 after a Supreme Court order, updated 2020).
- **How the manual works:** rainfall indicators come first, then impact indicators (sown area, satellite vegetation indices, soil moisture, reservoirs, groundwater), then field verification.
- **Relief:** the **SDRF**, topped up by the **NDRF** for severe calamities, under the **Disaster Management Act, 2005**.
- **Nodal ministry for drought:** the **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare**, not the MHA.

**Prelims traps**

1. ❌ "IMD declares drought." ✅ IMD only classifies rainfall. **States declare drought.**
2. ❌ "MHA is the nodal ministry for all disasters." ✅ For **drought** it is **Agriculture & Farmers Welfare**.
3. ❌ "87% of LPA is 'below normal'." ✅ It is **deficient**. Below normal is 90–95%.
4. ❌ "El Niño always means a deficient monsoon." ✅ A **positive IOD** can offset it (1997).
5. ❌ "−17% in a sub-division is deficient." ✅ For a sub-division, normal is **±19%**.

**Mains dimensions**

| Economic & Fiscal | Governance & Federal | Security & Geopolitics | Socio-Environmental |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kharif losses (pulses, oilseeds, groundnut) feed food inflation (see Pillar 2); rural demand falls; strain on SDRF | States declare, but money follows central norms, so states may hesitate. *Swaraj Abhiyan v. UoI* (2016) criticised states' reluctance to declare drought | Water stress sharpens inter-state river disputes; food stocks shape export curbs | Distress migration, fodder shortage, falling groundwater, women walking further for water |

**Way forward**

- ***Swaraj Abhiyan v. UoI* (2016):** the Court directed an updated drought manual, a **National Disaster Mitigation Fund**, and early, honest declarations.
- **Mihir Shah Committee (2016):** merge the CWC and CGWB into a single **National Water Commission**, shifting focus from building dams to managing aquifers and demand.
- **PMKSY "Per Drop More Crop"** micro-irrigation, plus crop diversification away from paddy in rain-shadow zones.
- Move from **relief to resilience**: risk-based financing and early declarations.

> **Telugu States Lens (APPSC / TGPSC Group-1)**
>
> - AP's 388 mandals were judged on rainfall *plus* dry spells, reservoirs, groundwater, soil moisture and vegetation. That is the drought manual in action.
> - **Rayalaseema**, especially undivided **Anantapur** district, is AP's most drought-prone belt.
> - **Telangana** ended about 17% short, which is still "normal" on the sub-division scale. The relevant state programme to cite is **Mission Kakatiya** (tank restoration).
> - *Use in:* the Group-1 Mains paper on economy and development.

## Pillar 2 — The Rate Hike `GS-3`

**Why in news**

- **September 2026 CPI inflation:** **5.54%**, up from **4.83%** in August.
  - Food inflation: **7.74%** (from 5.97%).
  - Rural **6.08%**, urban **4.88%**.
  - Onion prices more than doubled year on year (+108%).
  - Released 12 Oct 2026; new CPI series with **base year 2024**.
- **RBI MPC (5–7 Oct 2026):** repo rate raised **25 bps, from 5.25% to 5.50%**.
  - The **rate vote was 6–0**.
  - The stance moved from neutral to **"calibrated tightening"** (as reported) on a **4–2** vote.
  - **SDF 5.25%, MSF 5.75%.**
  - This is the **first hike since February 2023**.

**Static anchor: RBI Act, 1934 (amended 2016)**

| Section | What it says |
|---|---|
| **45ZA** | The **Central Government**, in consultation with the RBI, sets the inflation target every 5 years. The target is **4% CPI ± 2%**, **renewed on 25 Mar 2026 for 2026–31** |
| **45ZB** | Constitutes the **Monetary Policy Committee**: 6 members, 3 from the RBI (Governor chairs) and 3 external members appointed by the Centre |
| **45ZI** | MPC meetings and voting. On a **tie, the Governor has a second or casting vote** |
| **45ZN** | If the RBI **fails the target**, it must report the reasons and remedial action to the government. *Failure* is defined by the Centre's 2016 notification as average inflation outside 2–6% for **3 consecutive quarters** |

- **Urjit Patel Committee (2014)** recommended flexible inflation targeting.
- **Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) corridor:** the floor is the **SDF**, used since 2022 and needing no collateral. The ceiling is the **MSF**.

**Prelims traps**

1. ❌ "The RBI sets the inflation target." ✅ The **Central Government** does (s.45ZA).
2. ❌ "The MPC decides CRR and SLR." ✅ The MPC fixes **only the policy repo rate**.
3. ❌ "All MPC members are RBI officials." ✅ **Three are external**, appointed by the Centre.
4. ❌ "The reverse repo is the floor of the corridor." ✅ Since 2022 it has been the **SDF**.
5. ❌ "CPI is compiled by the RBI." ✅ By the **NSO under MoSPI**.

**Mains dimensions**

| Economic & Fiscal | Governance & Federal | Security & Geopolitics | Socio-Environmental |
|---|---|---|---|
| The repo rate is a demand tool, but this is a supply shock (drought, onion, crude). The hike aims to anchor **expectations** and stop **second-round effects**. Costs: higher EMIs, costlier MSME credit, slower growth | MPC independence: the 4–2 split shows open debate. Supply-side tools sit with the government | Crude oil and a weaker rupee import inflation | Inflation is a regressive tax: rural (6.08%) is above urban (4.88%). The drought taxes the poor twice |

**Way forward**

- Supply-side tools: the **Price Stabilisation Fund**, onion buffer stocks, **Operation Greens (TOP: tomato, onion, potato)**, and cold chains.
- **Coordination:** the RBI anchors expectations; the government fixes supply.
- **Link to Pillar 1:** drought-proofing agriculture is also anti-inflation policy.

> **Telugu States Lens:** **Kurnool** is AP's onion heartland. When prices swing, its farmers feel the crash and its shoppers feel the spike.

## Pillar 3 — Internet as a Fundamental Right, for Children Too `GS-2 · GS-4`

**Why in news**

- ***Just Rights for Children Alliance v. S. Harish***, judgment dated **15 Oct 2026**, by Justices **J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran**.
- **Holding:** the right to access and use the internet is a fundamental right of **every individual, including children**, under **Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a)**.
- **No blanket age bans.** The Court suggested proportionate safeguards instead: parental consent, a child mode, and private accounts by default. Modalities are left to the Union.
- The State may still block **inherently harmful content**, such as adult sites.
- **CSEAM directions:**
  - a **National Hash and URL List Database**, with entries checked by trained human analysts
  - quick takedown of matching content
  - the Union to consider exemplary fines
- *Our analysis:* age-verification systems that collect sensitive data can create new **privacy risks**.

**Static anchor: the case timeline**

| Year | Case | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | *K.S. Puttaswamy* | Privacy is a fundamental right; proportionality test |
| 2019 | *Faheema Shirin* (Kerala HC) | Internet access linked to the right to education (Art 21) |
| 2020 | *Anuradha Bhasin* | Speech and trade **through the internet** protected under Art 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g); shutdowns must be necessary and proportionate |
| 2024 (Sep) | *Just Rights for Children Alliance* | Viewing or storing CSEAM can attract **POCSO** and the **IT Act**; Court urged the term **CSEAM** |
| 2025 | *Pragya Prasun v. UoI* | **Digital access** is part of the right to life under **Art 21** |
| Oct 2026 | *Just Rights for Children Alliance* (judgment) | Right to **access and use** the internet, including for children |

- **DPDP Act, 2023:**
  - A **child is below 18**.
  - **Section 9** requires verifiable parental consent to process a child's data. This applies from about **May 2027**, 18 months after the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified.
- **Nodal ministries:** POCSO is with the **Ministry of Women & Child Development**; the IT Act and DPDP Act are with **MeitY**.

**Prelims traps**

1. ❌ "*Anuradha Bhasin* declared internet access a fundamental right." ✅ It protected rights exercised **through the medium** of the internet.
2. ❌ "The Oct 2026 judgment guarantees free internet for all." ✅ It is about **access and proportionate safeguards**. It does not, by itself, promise free internet.
3. ❌ "Under the DPDP Act, a child is below 16." ✅ Below **18**.
4. ❌ "POCSO is administered by MeitY." ✅ By the **Ministry of Women & Child Development**.

**Mains dimensions**

| Economic & Fiscal | Governance & Federal | Security & Geopolitics | Socio-Environmental |
|---|---|---|---|
| Education, DBT and UPI depend on access; platforms bear compliance costs | The Court sets the principle and leaves design to the executive, respecting the separation of powers. Fits with DPDP s.9 | CSEAM crosses borders; hash databases detect known material automatically | Digital divide by gender and geography; age-verification privacy risks |

**GS-4 lens:** paternalism vs. autonomy. *"Protection that removes the right to explore is not protection — it is confinement."* Prefer proportionality and safety-by-design over prohibition.

**Way forward**

- ***Justice B.N. Srikrishna Committee (2018)*** proposed special duties ("guardian data fiduciaries") for platforms handling children's data.
- A **safety-by-design** code for platforms instead of blanket bans.
- Amend POCSO terminology to **CSEAM**, as the Court recommended in 2024.
- **Privacy-preserving age assurance.**
- Close the access gap through **BharatNet** and **PM-WANI** public Wi-Fi.

## Pillar 4 — India–New Zealand FTA: Trade Without Dairy `GS-2 · GS-3`

**Why in news**

- **In force: 20 Oct 2026.** Signed **27 Apr 2026** in New Delhi; New Zealand passed its implementing law in **September 2026**.
- **Market access:** Indian exports get **duty-free access on all of New Zealand's tariff lines**. India offers concessions on about **70%** of its own lines.
- New Zealand commits to facilitating about **USD 20 billion** of investment.
- **Excluded:** milk, cream, whey, yogurt, cheese, onions, chickpeas, peas, almonds, sugar.
- **Apples, kiwifruit and manuka honey** get **lower duties only within fixed tariff-rate quotas**, and face **minimum import prices**. This protects growers in Himachal and Kashmir.

**Static anchor**

- **RCEP:** India **walked out in Nov 2019**, with fear of dairy import surges among the reasons. **New Zealand is an RCEP member.**
- **India's recent trade agreements:**
  - **Australia** ECTA (in force Dec 2022)
  - **EFTA** TEPA (in force Oct 2025; members **Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland**; USD 100 bn investment commitment)
  - **UK** CETA (signed July 2025)
- **WTO legality:** FTAs are permitted under **GATT Art XXIV** (goods) and **GATS Art V** (services).
- **Negotiator:** the **Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce & Industry**.

**Prelims traps**

1. ❌ "The MEA negotiates trade agreements." ✅ The **Department of Commerce**.
2. ❌ "EFTA is part of the EU." ✅ It is **not**.
3. ❌ "India is an RCEP member." ✅ India **withdrew in 2019**; NZ is a member.
4. ❌ "A tariff-rate quota is an import ban." ✅ It means a **lower duty inside the quota** and a higher duty above it.
5. ❌ "FTAs violate WTO rules." ✅ They are allowed under **GATT Art XXIV / GATS Art V**.

**Mains dimensions**

| Economic & Fiscal | Governance & Federal | Security & Geopolitics | Socio-Environmental |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access for labour-intensive exports (textiles, engineering, pharma); investment; low revenue risk | Agriculture is a State subject, but treaties use Union executive power without parliamentary ratification. *Debate:* is more parliamentary scrutiny needed? | The government presents each deal as a signal that India is a reliable partner after RCEP; supply-chain diversification; Pacific outreach | Dairy exclusion protects smallholder and women's livelihoods |

**Way forward**

- Describe the model as **"guarded openness"**: red lines, plus TRQs and minimum import prices instead of all-or-nothing choices. It answers the concerns India itself raised when it left RCEP in 2019 (protecting farmers and small industry).
- Give MSMEs **rules-of-origin support** so they can actually claim the lower duties.
- Use the deal as a template for larger negotiations.

## Mains Practice — 4 Questions

**M1. GS-3 · 15 marks · 250 words**
"A modest national rainfall deficit can conceal a severe regional drought." Examine with reference to the 2026 monsoon, and suggest how India's drought governance can shift from relief to resilience.

- *Intro:* the 13% national deficit vs. AP's −53% (June–August) and 388 drought mandals.
- *Body:* why averages mislead (spatial and temporal spread, dry spells, groundwater); institutional gaps (state reluctance, *Swaraj Abhiyan*, SDRF norms); resilience tools (multi-indicator manual, PMKSY, crop diversification).
- *Conclusion:* the Mihir Shah shift from dams to demand; risk-based disaster financing.

**M2. GS-3 · 10 marks · 150 words**
Can monetary tightening control inflation driven by supply shocks? Discuss with reference to the RBI's October 2026 policy.

- *Intro:* the 25 bps hike to 5.50%; September CPI 5.54%, food 7.74%.
- *Body:* limits of the rate tool (onion, drought, crude); why the RBI still acted (expectations, second-round effects, credibility of flexible inflation targeting).
- *Conclusion:* supply-side fixes (PSF, Operation Greens, buffer stocks) and fiscal–monetary coordination.

**M3. GS-2 · 15 marks · 250 words**
The Supreme Court has recognised the right to access and use the internet as a fundamental right, including for children, while rejecting blanket age-based bans. Critically examine how the State can balance child safety with digital freedom.

- *Intro:* the 15 Oct 2026 judgment and the line of cases from *Bhasin* to *Pragya Prasun*.
- *Body:* rights basis (Arts 21, 19(1)(a)); risks (CSEAM, data, online harm); proportionate tools (consent, child mode, hash database, DPDP s.9); separation-of-powers concerns.
- *Conclusion:* safety-by-design over prohibition; the Srikrishna Committee's approach.

**M4. GS-2 · 10 marks · 150 words**
India's FTA with New Zealand reflects a shift from defensive to guarded trade engagement. Discuss with reference to India's withdrawal from RCEP.

- *Intro:* in force 20 Oct 2026; the dairy red line.
- *Body:* why India left RCEP (dairy, agriculture, small industry); how this deal answers those concerns (exclusions, TRQs, minimum import prices); the gains (market access, investment).
- *Conclusion:* the remaining risks (low FTA utilisation, parliamentary scrutiny) and a template for future deals.
