## Script

### Part 1 · America proposes a 25% tariff on India

On November 25th, the office of the United States Trade Representative proposed a tariff of up to 25% on goods from India, under Section 301 of America's Trade Act of 1974. It says India's policies create "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing, which it calls unreasonable. There is a twist: if India and America sign a trade deal, the rate is capped at 17%. Nothing is in force yet. Public comments are open until January 5th, and a hearing is set for January 25th, 2027.

Why Section 301 now? Because in February this year, the United States Supreme Court struck down the earlier tariffs that were imposed under an emergency-powers law, called IEEPA. So Washington has moved to an older, narrower tool. Goods already covered under a different law, Section 232, like steel, aluminium, vehicles and semiconductors, are reported to be left out.

For India, America is our biggest export market, so this hits textiles, gems, engineering goods and more. India has disputed the finding, and the real question for Mains is this: can a country call another country's industrial policy, like Make in India, unfair, and punish it without going to the World Trade Organization?

Prelims trap: Section 301 is run by the US Trade Representative and is about unfair trade practices; Section 232 is run by the US Commerce Department and is about national security. Do not mix them.

Your first question: how many of these statements are correct?

> **Q1.** 1. Section 301 of the US Trade Act deals with unfair foreign trade practices. 2. Section 232 tariffs are justified on national-security grounds. 3. The proposed 25% tariff on India is already in force.  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. The tariff is only proposed; comments close on January 5th and the hearing is on January 25th.

### Part 2 · Nepal's Prime Minister comes to Delhi

On November 22nd and 23rd, Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah, popularly known as Balen, made his first state visit to India since taking office in March. The two countries signed 8 MoUs: on space, police and forensic training, civil-service training, health, traditional medicine, urban development and agriculture. India also announced help for Nepal's post-flood rebuilding and energy projects, reported at about 15,000 crore rupees: 3,500 crore as grants and 11,500 crore as a line of credit.

Now the static base. Our relationship rests on the Treaty of Peace and Friendship of 1950, which gives an open border and lets Nepali citizens live and work in India. The two rivers we share also have treaties: the Kosi in 1954, the Gandak in 1959 and the Mahakali in 1996, which plans the Pancheshwar project. And there is a sore point: the Kalapani, Lipulekh and Limpiyadhura area, which Nepal showed inside its own map in 2020.

Prelims trap: Nepal touches 5 Indian states: Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal and Sikkim. Students often forget Sikkim.

Question two: how many of these Indian states share a border with Nepal?

> **Q2.** 1. Uttarakhand 2. Himachal Pradesh 3. Sikkim 4. West Bengal  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) Only three · (d) All four

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is C, only three: Uttarakhand, Sikkim and West Bengal. Himachal Pradesh does not touch Nepal.

### Part 3 · COP31 ends with no deal

Last week we told you the climate summit in Antalya had a draft that dropped the fossil-fuel language. Here is the update: on November 22nd, COP31 ended without adopting that draft, the "Antalya Pact", at all. The European Union, Britain and several small island countries refused a text with no mention of fossil fuels, while oil producers refused to add it. This is the first time since Copenhagen in 2009 that a climate summit has closed without an overall political agreement.

So what survives? The Paris Agreement is untouched, and the Dubai decision of 2023, to transition away from fossil fuels, still stands. Smaller technical decisions were also adopted. And one correction from our side: in last week's video, the finance line should be "developed countries must deliver at least 300 billion dollars a year by 2035", the goal agreed at Baku.

For India, the stand stays the same: common but differentiated responsibilities, and finance from the developed world first.

Question three: which of these is correct?

> **Q3.** 1. COP31 adopted the Antalya Pact. 2. The 2023 Dubai decision to transition away from fossil fuels still stands. 3. COP31 was presided over by Türkiye, with Australia leading the negotiations.  
> (a) 1 and 2 only · (b) 2 and 3 only · (c) 1 and 3 only · (d) 1, 2 and 3

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, 2 and 3 only. The Antalya Pact was not adopted.

### Part 4 · Asia Power Index: India third

On November 22nd, Australia's Lowy Institute released its Asia Power Index for 2026. India kept the third place in Asia, behind the United States and China, with a reported score of about 40.5 out of 100. The index also says the gap between India and China is the narrowest since the index began in 2018, though China is still far ahead.

The index ranks 27 countries on 8 measures: economic capability, military capability, resilience, future resources, economic relationships, defence networks, diplomatic influence and cultural influence. India is strong on future resources and resilience, but weaker on economic relationships and defence networks.

Prelims trap: the Asia Power Index is published by the Lowy Institute, a think tank in Sydney, not by the United Nations or the World Bank.

Question four: consider these pairs.

> How many pairs are correctly matched?  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

The answer is A, only one. The World Happiness Report comes from the UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network and partners, and the Global Hunger Index from Concern Worldwide and Welthungerhilfe.

### Part 5 · Pakistan's first astronaut on China's space station

On November 25th, China launched Shenzhou-24 from Jiuquan on a Long March 2F rocket. On board was Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, Pakistan's first astronaut, flying as a payload specialist. He became the first foreign astronaut to visit China's own space station, Tiangong.

Why does it matter? Tiangong is China's own station, outside the International Space Station, so it shows China building its own space partnerships, and this one is with our neighbour.

Now line up India's record. Rakesh Sharma flew in 1984 on a Soviet Soyuz mission. In 2025, Shubhanshu Shukla became the first Indian on the International Space Station, on the Axiom-4 mission. And India's own Gaganyaan programme plans its first uncrewed test flight, with a Bharatiya Antariksh Station planned by 2035.

Prelims trap: China is not a partner of the International Space Station; its station is Tiangong. And here is a place trap: the Jiuquan launch centre is named after a city in Gansu, but it actually sits in Inner Mongolia.

Question five: how many of these statements are correct?

> **Q5.** 1. Tiangong is a part of the International Space Station. 2. Rakesh Sharma flew to space in 1984. 3. Shubhanshu Shukla was the first Indian to visit the International Space Station.  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. Tiangong is China's own station.

### Part 6 · The Strait of Hormuz and India's oil

This month, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said several oil tankers struck sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire; these claims could not be independently verified. Traffic through the Strait has dropped sharply, and the Indian Navy has been reported escorting India's tankers through the area.

Why does one narrow strait matter so much? The Strait of Hormuz lies between Iran in the north and Oman's Musandam peninsula in the south, and joins the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea. About a fifth of the world's oil passes through it, and India imports nearly 85% of its crude oil.

Now the Telugu States Lens. India keeps an emergency oil stock in underground rock caverns, and the very first of them is in our Visakhapatnam, holding 1.33 million tonnes. The other two are at Mangaluru and Padur in Karnataka, for a total of 5.33 million tonnes, run by Indian Strategic Petroleum Reserves Limited.

Prelims trap: Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman; Bab-el-Mandeb connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden; and Malacca connects the Indian Ocean to the South China Sea.

### Part 7 · Made-in-India machines for building India

On November 25th, the Union Cabinet approved an 11,000-crore-rupee scheme to make construction and infrastructure equipment in India: tunnel boring machines, ship-to-shore cranes, hydraulic excavators and heavy off-road trucks. The Ministry of Heavy Industries will run it for 8 years from 2026-27. Makers get an incentive linked to their extra sales each year, and up to 375 crore rupees will help set up a national testing facility.

Why now? India's construction-equipment industry is already the third largest in the world, after China and America, but we import many of the biggest machines, mostly from China. China has also started asking for export licences for tunnel boring machines, the machines that dig our metro and highway tunnels.

Prelims trap: this scheme is run by the Ministry of Heavy Industries, not the Ministry of Commerce, and its incentive is tied to incremental sales, the same idea as the Production Linked Incentive schemes.

Question seven: which ministry implements the new construction-equipment scheme?

> **Q7.** (a) Ministry of Commerce and Industry · (b) Ministry of Heavy Industries · (c) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways · (d) Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, the Ministry of Heavy Industries.

### Part 8 · Nitish Setu: India's longest river bridge

On November 21st, the Prime Minister opened Nitish Setu in Bihar, a 6-lane bridge over the Ganga that is 9.76 kilometres long. It joins Kachchi Dargah near Patna with Bidupur in Vaishali district, and with its approach roads the corridor runs to about 19.76 kilometres. It is reported to be India's longest river bridge.

So let us line up the bridge records, because examiners love look-alikes. Bhupen Hazarika Setu over the Lohit in Assam, about 9.15 kilometres, was the longest river bridge until now. Bogibeel over the Brahmaputra near Dibrugarh is India's longest rail-cum-road bridge. Atal Setu in Mumbai, about 21.8 kilometres, is India's longest sea bridge. And Mahatma Gandhi Setu between Patna and Hajipur held the river record from 1982 to 2017.

Question eight: how many pairs are correctly matched?

> **Q8.** 1. Bogibeel — Brahmaputra 2. Bhupen Hazarika Setu — Lohit 3. Atal Setu — Hooghly  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. Atal Setu crosses the sea at Mumbai, not the Hooghly.

### Part 9 · Constitution Day, Milk Day and 26/11

November 26th was Constitution Day, or Samvidhan Divas, observed since 2015. It marks the day in 1949 when the Constituent Assembly adopted our Constitution; this year was the 77th anniversary. The full Constitution came into force on January 26th, 1950, but some articles, like those on citizenship and elections, started on November 26th, 1949 itself.

The same day is National Milk Day, the birthday of Dr Verghese Kurien, born in 1921, the father of the White Revolution, who led Operation Flood and built the Amul cooperative model at Anand.

And November 26th also marks 18 years since the 2008 Mumbai terror attacks. After that attack, India created the National Investigation Agency through the NIA Act of 2008 and strengthened its coastal security.

Prelims trap: adopted on November 26th, 1949; fully in force on January 26th, 1950. Do not swap them.

Question nine: consider these statements.

> **Q9.** 1. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949. 2. No provision of the Constitution came into force before 26 January 1950. 3. National Milk Day marks the birth anniversary of Verghese Kurien.  
> (a) 1 and 2 only · (b) 1 and 3 only · (c) 2 and 3 only · (d) 1, 2 and 3

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, 1 and 3 only. Citizenship and election provisions came into force on November 26th, 1949 itself.

### Part 10 · Two heroes of November 24th

November 24th was Lachit Divas, the 404th birth anniversary of Lachit Borphukan, the Ahom general born in 1622. In 1671, he defeated a much larger Mughal army in the Battle of Saraighat, a battle fought on the Brahmaputra near Guwahati. Today, the best cadet of the National Defence Academy receives the Lachit Borphukan Gold Medal.

The same date is also observed as the martyrdom day of Guru Tegh Bahadur, the 9th Sikh Guru, who was executed in Delhi in 1675 on the orders of Aurangzeb, for defending the freedom of faith. Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib in Chandni Chowk marks the spot, and he is remembered as "Hind di Chadar", the shield of India.

Prelims trap: Guru Tegh Bahadur was the 9th Guru; his son, the 10th Guru, Guru Gobind Singh, founded the Khalsa in 1699.

Question 10: how many pairs are correctly matched?

> **Q10.** 1. Battle of Saraighat — Lachit Borphukan 2. Guru Tegh Bahadur — founded the Khalsa 3. Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib — Delhi  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. The Khalsa was founded by Guru Gobind Singh, the 10th Guru.

### Part 11 · Two years of a child-marriage-free India

November 27th marked two years of the Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat campaign, launched by the Ministry of Women and Child Development in 2024. Its aim is to cut child marriage by 10% by 2026 and to make India free of child marriage by 2030.

The law behind it is the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act of 2006. It sets the age of marriage at 18 for girls and 21 for boys, and every state appoints Child Marriage Prohibition Officers to stop such marriages. A bill to raise the age for girls to 21 was introduced in 2021, but it lapsed when the 17th Lok Sabha was dissolved.

Question 11: under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, what is the minimum age of marriage?

> **Q11.** (a) 18 for both · (b) 21 for both · (c) 18 for girls and 21 for boys · (d) 16 for girls and 18 for boys

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is C: 18 for girls and 21 for boys.
