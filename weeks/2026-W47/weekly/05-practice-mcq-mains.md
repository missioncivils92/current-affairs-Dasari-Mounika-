## Mains (GS-2 · 15 marks · 250 words)

For the first time, the International Court of Justice has held a State responsible for genocide. Discuss the significance of the Myanmar judgment for international law and its implications for India.

**Answer outline:**
- **Introduction:** the 19 Nov 2026 judgment (9–6): Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017. It is the first time the ICJ has found a State itself responsible (compare Bosnia v. Serbia, 2007, which found only a failure to prevent).
- **For international law:**
  - *erga omnes* standing: any party to the 1948 Convention can sue (The Gambia, backed by the OIC);
  - remedies of restitution and compensation, with a duty to report;
  - a standard of proof for intent that future cases will test;
  - the limits: no enforcement except through the UN Security Council.
- **For India:**
  - India ratified the Convention in 1959 but has no domestic genocide law;
  - the Rohingya refugee question (India is not party to the 1951 Convention; non-refoulement debate);
  - India–Myanmar ties: a 1,643 km border, the Kaladan project and the Act East Policy;
  - stability in the north-east;
  - balancing values and strategic interest.
- **Conclusion:** support a rules-based order and humanitarian access while engaging Myanmar pragmatically; strengthen national law and refugee policy.
