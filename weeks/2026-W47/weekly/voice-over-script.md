## Script


### Part 1 · The world court finds genocide

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story one: why is the world court's judgment on Myanmar a first in history?…"*

On November 19th, the International Court of Justice ruled, by nine votes to six, that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during its 2017 military campaign in Rakhine State, in which about 10,000 Rohingya were killed. It is the first time the court has found a state itself responsible for genocide, and it ordered Myanmar to begin a programme of restitution and compensation and to report back within two years.

How did a small African country bring this case? The Gambia filed it in 2019, on behalf of the 57 members of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation. It could do so because the Genocide Convention of 1948 lets any party take another party to the world court, even if it was not harmed itself.

Now the exam angle for India. India ratified the Genocide Convention in 1959, and it hosts Rohingya refugees, though it is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention. So this judgment touches our neighbourhood, our refugee policy and our relations with Myanmar, which shares a sixteen-hundred-kilometre border with our north-east.

Prelims trap: the International Court of Justice judges states, not individuals; individuals are tried by the International Criminal Court, and India is not a member of that court.

ఇది గుర్తుపెట్టుకోండి.

Your first question: how many of these three statements are correct?

> **Q1.** 1. The ICJ has 15 judges elected for 9-year terms. 2. The ICJ can try individuals for genocide. 3. India is a party to the Genocide Convention.  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. The world court has 15 judges with nine-year terms, and India is a party to the Genocide Convention; but the court cannot try individuals.


### Part 2 · America, China and Russia at one table

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story two: the leaders of the three biggest nuclear powers met together for the …"*

On November 19th, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held their first-ever three-way summit in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation leaders' meeting. Their joint declaration promised a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and stopping the spread of nuclear weapons, but it set no deadline and no disarmament target.

Why does this matter? The last treaty limiting American and Russian nuclear weapons, New START, lapsed in February this year, so for now no treaty caps the world's two largest arsenals. A three-way dialogue that includes China is new, but it is only a promise to talk.

For India, all three are vital: America for technology and the Quad, Russia for defence and oil, and China as our biggest neighbour and a trading partner. When the three talk directly, India must keep its strategic autonomy and its own seat at the table.

Prelims trap: the Non-Proliferation Treaty recognises only five nuclear-weapon states, America, Russia, Britain, France and China; India is not a party to it, nor to the test-ban treaty, and follows a policy of no first use.

Question two: how many of these countries are recognised as nuclear-weapon states under the Non-Proliferation Treaty?

> **Q2.** Consider: 1. China 2. India 3. France 4. Israel. How many are recognised nuclear-weapon states under the NPT?  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) Only three · (d) All four

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two: China and France. India and Israel are not recognised nuclear-weapon states under the treaty.

In the same visit, the Prime Minister attended the 23rd ASEAN–India Summit and the 21st East Asia Summit, which keeps our Act East Policy at the centre of the Indo-Pacific.

> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) Only three · (d) All four

### Part 4 · India says no to a death-penalty pause

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story four: why did India vote against a United Nations call to pause the death …"*

On November 13th, the United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution calling for a global moratorium on the death penalty, with 133 votes in favour, 33 against and 16 abstentions. India voted against it, as it has every time, saying there is no international consensus and that each country has the sovereign right to decide its own laws.

At home, the death penalty survives within strict limits. Article 21 allows the state to take life only by a procedure established by law, and in Bachan Singh in 1980, the Supreme Court said it should be used only in the rarest of rare cases. After that, the President under Article 72 and the Governor under Article 161 can still grant pardon.

Prelims trap: only the President can pardon a sentence passed by a court-martial; the Governor's power under Article 161 does not extend to court-martial sentences.

ఇది గుర్తుపెట్టుకోండి.

Question four: consider these two statements and choose the right option.

> **Q4.** Statement-I: The President can pardon a sentence passed by a court-martial. Statement-II: Article 72 extends the President's pardoning power to court-martial sentences.  
> (a) Both correct, II explains I · (b) Both correct, II does not explain I · (c) I correct, II incorrect · (d) I incorrect, II correct

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is A: both are correct, and Article 72 is exactly why the President can pardon a court-martial sentence.


### Part 5 · One year of the Labour Codes

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story five: India's new labour codes complete one year on November 21st. What ch…"*

On November 21st, 2025, four labour codes came into force and replaced 29 central labour laws: the Code on Wages, the Industrial Relations Code, the Code on Social Security, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code.

Workers gain a national floor wage, and, for the first time, gig and platform workers are defined in law and brought under social security. Employers gain simpler compliance and fixed-term hiring. Some provisions are still being phased in, so the real test is how states write and enforce their rules.

Prelims trap: labour is in the Concurrent List, so both Parliament and the states can make laws on it, and states must frame their own rules under these codes.

Question five: how many of these statements are correct?

> **Q5.** 1. Four labour codes replaced 29 central labour laws. 2. Labour is in the Union List. 3. The Code on Social Security covers gig and platform workers.  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. Labour is in the Concurrent List, not the Union List.


### Part 6 · A week of defence diplomacy

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story six: in one week, India held defence talks or exercises with four partners…"*

With Japan, the defence and foreign ministers held the fourth two-plus-two dialogue in Tokyo. With Malaysia, the armies began Harimau Shakti, focused on jungle warfare and counter-insurgency. With Sri Lanka, the 12th Mitra Shakti exercise is under way, focused on counter-terrorism. And with Israel, the Defence Ministry signed a contract of about 1,976 crore rupees to upgrade the Navy's Barak missile defence.

A two-plus-two means foreign and defence ministers meeting together, a format India keeps for its closest partners, and with Japan it links straight to our Act East Policy and the Indo-Pacific.

Question six: how many of these exercise pairs are correctly matched?

> **Q6.** 1. Harimau Shakti — India–Malaysia 2. Mitra Shakti — India–Sri Lanka 3. JAIMEX — India–Japan (maritime) 4. Garuda Shakti — India–Malaysia  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) Only three · (d) All four

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is C, only three. Garuda Shakti is with Indonesia, not Malaysia.


### Part 7 · Cyclone Akvan crosses our coast

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story seven: Cyclone Akvan crossed the north Andhra and south Odisha coast on No…"*

It crossed close to Gopalpur in Odisha's Ganjam district, with a red alert for Srikakulam and Vizianagaram and more than 3 lakh people moved to safety.

The name Akvan was given by Iran, from a demon in the Persian epic, the Shahnameh. 13 countries gave 169 names in 2020, used strictly in order, and India's weather office in New Delhi assigns them. Storms are graded by wind speed: a cyclonic storm starts at 62 kilometres an hour, a severe one at 89, and a very severe one at 118.

Prelims trap: Yanam sits on the Andhra coast, but it belongs to Puducherry, not Andhra Pradesh.

Question seven: how many of these statements are correct?

> **Q7.** 1. Cyclone names are chosen according to the country a storm hits. 2. 'Akvan' was proposed by Iran. 3. Yanam is a part of Puducherry.  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is B, only two. Names are used in a fixed order, not by the country a storm hits.


### Part 8 · Telugu States: the Nagarjuna Sagar water row

⏭ *On camera (skip): "ఇప్పుడు మన Telugu states story.…"*

Nagarjuna Sagar sits on the Krishna, on the border of Telangana's Nalgonda and Andhra Pradesh's Palnadu district, and both states draw from it. This week, officials of the two states clashed over water releases, and with a dry year ahead, every drop for drinking water is being argued over.

Who decides? The Krishna River Management Board, set up under the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act of 2014, manages releases. The shares come from tribunals: the first, under Justice Bachawat, gave its award in 1976, and the second is headed by Justice Brijesh Kumar. And under Article 262, Parliament can even keep the courts out of such disputes.

Question eight, for Group-1 too: how many of these statements are correct?

> **Q8.** 1. The Krishna River Management Board was set up under the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014. 2. The first Krishna tribunal was headed by Justice Bachawat. 3. Under Article 262, Parliament may exclude the Supreme Court's jurisdiction over inter-state water disputes.  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) All three · (d) None

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is C, all three are correct.

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story nine: the Earthshot Prize came to India for the first time, and an Indian …"*

The prize was launched in 2020 by Prince William and his Royal Foundation, and every year it gives 1 million pounds each to five winners. Its sixth edition was held in Mumbai on November 17th.

Mahindra Last Mile Mobility won Clean Our Air for affordable electric autos, with more than 4 lakh on Indian roads.

The others were Bhutan, the first country ever to win, the Pacific island of Niue for ocean protection, a French campaign against forever chemicals, and Denmark's Ørsted for moving from oil and gas to offshore wind.

Prelims trap: the Earthshot Prize is not a United Nations award; the UN's own top green honour is Champions of the Earth, given by the UN Environment Programme.

### Part 10 · COP31 ends in Antalya

⏭ *On camera (skip): "Story 10: the climate summit in Türkiye reached its final day. Who was running i…"*

The 31st Conference of the Parties met in Antalya from November 9th to 20th, with an unusual split: Türkiye hosted and held the presidency, while Australia's Chris Bowen led the negotiations. Every decision needs the agreement of nearly 200 countries.

In the final hours, the latest draft, called the Antalya Declaration, dropped every mention of moving away from fossil fuels and a proposed target of 120 billion dollars for adaptation, which disappointed more than 80 countries that wanted a fossil-fuel roadmap. India's position stayed clear: developed countries must deliver the finance they promised at Baku, at least 300 billion dollars a year by 2035.

Question 10: how many pairs are correctly matched?

> **Q10.** 1. COP21 — Paris 2. COP29 — Baku 3. COP30 — Belém 4. COP31 — Australia  
> (a) Only one · (b) Only two · (c) Only three · (d) All four

*(pause 2 seconds)*

The answer is C, only three. COP31 was held in Antalya, Türkiye; Australia only led the negotiations.
