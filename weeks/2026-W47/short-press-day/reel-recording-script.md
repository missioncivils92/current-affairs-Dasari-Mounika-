## Script

Today is National Press Day, and it does not celebrate a newspaper. It celebrates the watchdog that watches over newspapers! 

ఆ watchdog ఎవరు?

 Let's decode.

On November 16th, 1966, the Press Council of India began its work. It was recommended by the First Press Commission, which reported in 1954, and it was set up under the Press Council Act of 1965.

But here is a twist. During the Emergency, in 1976, the Council was abolished. It came back under a new law, the Press Council Act of 1978, and that is the law in force today.

It has a chairperson and 28 members, and by convention the chairperson is a retired Supreme Court judge. It is a statutory, quasi-judicial body: it can warn, admonish or censure a newspaper, but it cannot fine it or punish a journalist.

Prelims trap: the Press Council covers print media only, not television and not digital news. And press freedom has no separate Article: it comes from freedom of speech and expression, Article 19(1)(a). 

ఇది గుర్తుపెట్టుకోండి!

Mains question: in an age of fake news, should the Press Council get the power to penalise, and should it cover television and digital news too?
