## Script

You have a PAN card, a voter card and an Aadhaar card, so you are surely an Indian citizen, right? The Gauhati High Court says, not so fast! 

ఎందుకు?

 Let's decode.

In a recent ruling, the Gauhati High Court said that a PAN card and a voter card, issued on a person's own declaration, cannot by themselves prove citizenship, unless the records behind them are proved. It has taken the same line in earlier cases too, including one this July.

So how does one become an Indian citizen? The Constitution covers the beginning, in Articles 5 to 11, and Article 11 lets Parliament make the law. That law, the Citizenship Act of 1955, gives five ways: birth, descent, registration, naturalisation and incorporation of territory.

Why Assam? Under the Assam Accord, Section 6A of the Act uses a cut-off date of March 25th, 1971, and in 2024 a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court upheld it. Such cases go to Foreigners Tribunals, and the burden of proof lies on the person, not on the state.

Prelims trap: by itself, none of these proves citizenship: not the PAN card, not the voter card, and not Aadhaar either, because the Aadhaar Act itself says so.

Mains question: how should the state balance the need to identify foreigners with the right of genuine citizens not to be harassed?
