## 2. UPSC CSE: structure we must respect

**Stage 1: Prelims (objective, screening only)**
- GS Paper I: 100 questions, 200 marks, 2 hours, −1/3 negative marking. This paper decides the cut-off.
- CSAT (GS Paper II): 80 questions, 200 marks, qualifying at 33%.

**Stage 2: Mains (descriptive, 1750 merit marks)**
- Qualifying papers: Indian Language (300) and English (300), 25% each.
- Merit papers: Essay (250), GS-I (250), GS-II (250), GS-III (250), GS-IV Ethics (250), Optional I + II (250 + 250).

**Stage 3: Personality Test, 275 marks.** Grand total: 2025.

**Eligibility snapshot:** age 21–32 for General, with relaxations for OBC, SC/ST and PwBD. Attempt limits are 6 for General/EWS and 9 for OBC; SC/ST attempts are unlimited within the age limit.

### Key numbers, CSE 2026 cycle **[verify]**
- Notification: 4 Feb 2026, about 933 vacancies (one report cites 1,016 in the result press note).
- Prelims: 24 May 2026. About 8.19 lakh registered, 5.49 lakh appeared, 13,343 qualified for Mains.

## 3. What the recent papers tell us about content strategy

**Prelims GS-I is volatile on "pure" current affairs but steady on current-linked static topics.**
- In the 2025 paper, coaching analyses counted about 22–23 explicitly current-affairs questions (VisionIAS, PW). Economy, Environment and Geography each had about 12–15.
- For 2026, analyses disagree sharply. Counts of pure current affairs range from 3 to 15. Most agree the paper was lengthy, factual, statement-heavy, and stronger on History, S&T and International Relations.
- **Implication:** a current-affairs video that only reports the news is low value. Every issue must be pushed back into its static base: the Article, the Act, the species, the treaty clause, the geography. The **Prelims Trap Vault** step stays mandatory.
- Question format trend: multi-statement questions ("How many of the above…"), assertion–reason pairs, and fewer match-the-following items. MCQs we write must copy these formats.

**Mains rewards analysis over recall.**
- GS-II 2026: about 10 Polity, 3 Social Justice, 3 Governance and 4 IR questions. It is the most current-affairs-driven paper, built on the last 12–18 months of developments with the Constitution as the analytical frame.
- GS-III 2026 themes: Digital Rupee (CBDC), RBI Financial Inclusion Index, agri-tech missions and agricultural marketing, e-commerce in agriculture, WTO subsidies, fast breeder reactors, biotechnology, Kunming–Montreal biodiversity framework, disaster-risk reduction, IT Rules and fake news, cyber threats, counterfeit currency, money laundering and FATF.
- **Implication:** the four-dimension Mains matrix and the committee-backed Way Forward are exactly what the exam rewards. Every topic should end with a model Mains question and a 3-part answer skeleton.

## 4. APPSC & TGPSC Group-1: structure **[verify all]**

**APPSC Group-1:** Prelims screening (2 objective papers), then descriptive Mains, then interview. Mains has qualifying Telugu and English papers, plus Essay and four GS papers covering History & Culture (India + AP), Polity, Constitution, Governance, Law & Ethics, Economy & Development (India + AP), and S&T and Environment.

**TGPSC Group-1:** Prelims screening (GS & Mental Ability), then descriptive Mains with General English (qualifying) and six papers: Essay; History, Culture & Geography; Indian Society, Constitution & Governance; Economy & Development; S&T & Data Interpretation; Telangana Movement & State Formation.

**Implication:** about 70% of Group-1 content overlaps with UPSC GS. The extra value we add is a short **"Telugu States Lens"** segment where a topic has a real AP/Telangana link: Polavaram, Krishna–Godavari water sharing, AP Reorganisation Act 2014 disputes, Amaravati, Hyderabad's semiconductor and pharma clusters, state fiscal health, state welfare schemes, Nallamala and Eastern Ghats biodiversity, and similar.

## 5. Competitive landscape on YouTube

- Large incumbents (StudyIQ, Drishti IAS, Vision IAS, PW OnlyIAS, Vajiram & Ravi) run **daily** newspaper analysis and weekly roundups. They are mostly Hindi or bilingual, and their production is dense and lecture-heavy.
- Our gap: a **single cinematic weekly masterclass** in English, with UPSC-grade depth, a visible exam lens on every segment (Prelims trap, Mains frame, state angle), and premium visuals instead of slide decks.
- Trust matters: one major channel was penalised by the consumer regulator (Dec 2024) over misleading result claims **[verify]**. The channel never makes selection or topper claims it cannot document.
