# Cap the Sunday Hub, Spin Off Depth

*Research report for Decode UPSC with Mounika. Compiled 27 October 2026. Question: what length and structure the weekly masterclass should have, how to fit a full week's content, and what release plan a single narrator can sustain.*

**Evidence caveat.** Direct page fetches were blocked for this whole session. YouTube, Telegram, support.google.com, blog.youtube, Tubefilter, Retention Rabbit, PMC, Frontiers and ASEE PEER all failed. Nearly every external finding below therefore rests on **search-engine summaries and snippets, not full-text reads**. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** need a direct check before they go into an owner decision memo or on air. No competitor video durations could be confirmed. The only hard numbers in this report that come from a primary source are the channel's own: the Oleshky sample's word counts and runtime.

**Bottom line.** Make the Sunday masterclass a **hub episode of 28–38 minutes**, normally about 32 minutes. Treat **40 minutes as an overflow trigger, not a hard cap**: above it, content gets rerouted rather than squeezed in. Each Deep Dive inside the hub gets **5–8 minutes (about 700–1,100 words at 135 wpm)**. The Oleshky sample shows the tension. Its Deep Dive section alone is **1,571 words, about 11.6 minutes at Mounika's pace**, and the whole sample is **1,914 words, about 14 minutes**. Three or four pillars at that depth add up to the 40–60 minutes the owner is worried about. The fix is not to rush the narration. The fix is a **modular script**: every Deep Dive is written as a hub core plus removable extension blocks (an extended timeline, the examiner's chair, the full Mains proposition, the model-answer walk-through). The hub uses the core. When a pillar honestly needs 10–15 minutes, the full version ships as **one standalone "Decode Extended" upload on Wednesday**. Shorts (3–5 a week) carry single Glimpses and traps for reach. The monthly compilation, rebuilt from existing renders, is the spaced second viewing. The evidence supports this shape on three points. Learning research supports **segmenting and frequent retrieval**, not a short total runtime. YouTube's stated position is that satisfaction, not length, drives recommendation. And for a solo operation, the binding constraint is **editing hours, not the narrator's voice**. A second recording session per week fits easily, but a second fully built 40-minute video does not.

## The Oleshky sample is a 14-minute pillar in a 7-minute slot

The arithmetic decides most of this question, so it comes first. The sample Oleshky video's final cut runs **11:56**, with 11:32 of ElevenLabs narration, from a **1,914-word** master script. That works out to about 166 wpm. At the channel's rule of 135 wpm, the same script runs **about 14:10** (13:40–14:45 across the 130–140 wpm band). The script itself logs this (`weeks/2026-W44/sample-oleshky/02-master-script.md`). Strip out the cold open, the "Previously on Decode" update and the verdict, and the Deep Dive alone is **1,571 words, about 11.6 minutes** at 135 wpm. That is **45–130% over** the current 5–8 minute Deep Dive budget in CLAUDE.md §3.1.

The sample is not padded. It runs long because it carries every element of the 5-step analysis: a key-terms primer, a map, a "timeline river" from 1954 to the 2023 dam breach, a fragility mind map, India's dilemma as a split screen, a full Prelims Trap Vault, a PYQ Echo with the examiner's chair, a four-quadrant Mains Lens, a contestable proposition argued both ways, a Way Forward, a model 15-mark question with its skeleton, and a recap with a fill-in-the-blanks map. Run three or four pillars at that density, add Focus segments and a News Grid, and the week lands at 50–60 minutes. So the owner's estimate is right.

The useful insight is that these elements are **not equally essential to a first viewing**. The key-terms primer, map, mind map, Trap Vault, a one-paragraph Mains Lens and the checkpoint carry the exam value. The extended timeline, the examiner's-chair dramatisation, the two-sided proposition and the model-answer walk-through deepen it. They also duplicate what the weekly PDF already provides: one page per pillar with the Mains questions and skeletons, as PLAYBOOK §Saturday specifies. A hub-length Oleshky at about 7:30 needs about **1,010 words, a cut of roughly 35%**. That cut comes almost entirely from moving the proposition and the model-answer walk-through to the PDF and the extended cut, folding the examiner's chair into the Trap Vault card, and shortening the timeline to its three load-bearing dates. Nothing taught in the Trap Vault is lost.

## Learning science sets the segment length, not the total length

The most-quoted length finding is the "6-minute rule". It comes from Guo, Kim and Rubin's 2014 analysis of **6.9 million edX viewing sessions**, which found that "shorter videos are much more engaging" ([Guo et al. 2014 record](https://www.bibsonomy.org/bibtex/2ea6e7d9bcdcfedf406d8c9843c5d5687/aho)). Secondary summaries report median engagement topping out at about 6 minutes, falling to about **50% for 9–12-minute videos and about 20% for 12–40-minute videos** ([UBC summary](https://flexible.learning.ubc.ca/?p=2852)) **[UNVERIFIED against full text]**. This is an **engagement** finding about low-stakes MOOC learners, not a **learning** finding. NYU's commentary notes that the study "did not examine the impact of videos on student learning" ([NYU SPS](https://nexus.sps.nyu.edu/post/the-six-minute-myth)). Lagerstrom and colleagues looked at working professionals taking for-credit courses and called the rule a "myth" ([ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/the-myth-of-the-six-minute-rule-student-engagement-with-online-videos)). Bradbury's review found **no primary evidence for a fixed 10–15-minute attention limit**. Attention varied more with the teacher than with the format, and the "8-second goldfish" figure has no measurement behind it ([Bradbury 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28145268/)). UPSC and Group-1 aspirants on a weekly "replace your daily videos" promise resemble Lagerstrom's motivated professionals far more than casual MOOC browsers.

What does hold up is **segmenting**. A meta-analysis of 56 investigations found small-to-medium gains in retention and transfer when a lesson is broken into segments, even when the system sets the pace ([Rey et al. 2019](https://is.tuebingen.mpg.de/publications/rey2018segmenting)) **[effect sizes not retrieved]**. Retrieval between segments matters too. In a roughly 21-minute online lecture, brief interpolated tests "reduced mind wandering, increased note-taking and improved learning" ([Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/)). In a **40-minute lecture cut into eight segments**, interpolated tests still improved learning and the integration of ideas across segments, though overall mind-wandering no longer fell significantly ([Jing, Szpunar & Schacter 2016](https://sites.harvard.edu/schacter-memory/files/2022/08/jing2016-1-1.pdf)). That 40-minute, 5-minutes-per-segment design is the best-evidenced long-video structure found, and it maps directly onto the masterclass: 5–8-minute Deep Dive chapters, 1.5–3-minute Focus chapters, and a "Pause and answer" checkpoint at the end of each pillar. It is also why this report puts the overflow trigger at **40 minutes**. Beyond that point there is no evidence that the testing scaffold still holds attention.

Two behavioural facts lower the real cost of a long hub. First, speed-watching is the norm. In the UCLA work, **85% of students usually watched lectures faster than normal**, and 1.5x–2x showed minimal comprehension cost for simple material ([BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/watching-lecture-twice-double-speed-can-benefit-learning)). Two 2025 meta-analyses do find small-to-moderate costs to retention and transfer from accelerated playback ([Huang et al. 2025, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1427609/full)) **[secondary]**. A 32-minute hub watched at 1.25x takes about 26 minutes. The slower 110–120 wpm rows on numbers and traps protect exactly the content most at risk, and checkpoint cards should hold for **6–8 seconds**, so that a 2x viewer still gets 3–4 seconds. Second, standard aspirant guidance sets aside a **2–3-hour weekly current-affairs consolidation slot** ([CareerIndia](https://www.careerindia.com/news/upsc-current-affairs-weekly-revision-strategy-2026-prelims-mains-guide-056377.html)). A 30–38-minute hub plus the PDF and MCQs (about 60–90 minutes of total engagement) fills roughly half that slot. That leaves room for the aspirant's own revision, which is the "alongside one newspaper" promise. A 60-minute hub would eat most of the slot and break the promise.

## YouTube rewards satisfaction, so the hub's job is to keep people, not minutes

YouTube's recommendations page says the system learns from "over 80 billion" signals and added watch time in 2012 because clicks alone misled ([How YouTube Works](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations)). Cristos Goodrow of YouTube has said **satisfaction is the top objective**, and that the system "might go with watch time for one [user], and user retention for another" ([Tubefilter, Sept 2021](https://tubefilter.com/2021/09/15/youtube-creator-insider-recommendation-algorithm-faqs/)). Todd Beaupré's advice, quoted second-hand, is to stop "stretching out those videos" and focus on "maximizing the value" ([vidIQ summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/youtube-launches-new-algorithm-updates-2025)) **[UNVERIFIED wording]**. Rene Ritchie's line is that a video should be "as long as it needs to be, but no longer" ([ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-long-can-a-youtube-video-be-in-2026-best-length-guide/)) **[secondary]**. No official source says that length, or upload frequency, is a ranking factor in itself.

The retention benchmarks are generic, not specific to UPSC. The Retention Rabbit 2025 dataset reportedly puts average retention at **23.7%**, finds that **55% of viewers are gone by the 60-second mark**, and puts educational how-tos near **42%** ([TubeLab, repeating the report](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention)) **[UNVERIFIED; report page unreachable]**. The same guide gives typical retention of **25–35% for 20+ minute videos** ([TubeLab](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention)). For a 32-minute hub, that implies an average view duration of roughly 8–11 minutes. The baseline should be replaced with the channel's own Studio data after 4–6 uploads. Two consequences follow. The first 60 seconds matter more than the 32nd minute, which reinforces the existing rule to start the cold open inside the event, pose the pre-question and drop "Welcome back". And per-chapter retention, not total length, is the metric to manage.

Chapters make a long hub work. YouTube requires a 00:00 first timestamp, at least three timestamps and chapters of at least 10 seconds ([YouTube Help 9884579](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579?hl=en)). YouTube chapters also feed Google Search "key moments" automatically ([Google Search Central, July 2021](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/07/new-way-key-moments)). So one hub with question-titled chapters surfaces for several topical searches, which recovers much of the discovery advantage of splitting into separate uploads. Chapters can lower raw average view duration because viewers jump around ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-chapters-key-moments/)). For a revision product, that skipping is the point, so returning viewers and session time are better measures than AVD.

Competitor benchmarks are thin. The UPSC niche splits into short daily or topic videos (Sleepy Classes recaps run about **5–17 minutes** ([Telegram snapshot](https://t.me/s/SleepyClasses?before=13411)) **[low confidence]**) and multi-hour monthly marathons. Vision IAS lists its monthly revision class at **3–4 hours on one page and 5–6 hours on another** ([Vision IAS MCAR](https://beta.visionias.in/mcar)). Drishti and StudyIQ run weekly formats, but their lengths could not be confirmed ([Drishti archive](https://www.drishtiias.com/hindi/gallery/archive?page=74)). A tightly built 30–38-minute English weekly therefore sits in an **uncrowded middle band**. That is an inference from absent data, not proof.

## One hub, at most one extended cut, Shorts for reach

Four release architectures were weighed. A **single long video of 45–60 minutes** keeps everything in one place but runs past the 40-minute evidence and the weekly time budget. It also asks too much of new viewers on a small channel. **Splitting every Deep Dive into standalone uploads** multiplies titles and thumbnails, but it duplicates content in front of the same subscribers, multiplies packaging and re-timing work, and turns a "course" into a feed. No data was found on whether standalone re-cuts suppress the parent video's recommendations, and that gap is reason enough not to do it by default. **Shorts as the main funnel** is weak: vendor-reported click-through on the Related Video link is "sometimes under 1%" ([Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-not-converting-viewers)) **[vendor, UNVERIFIED]**, and a 5,400-Short study concluded that Shorts "aren't a silver bullet for growth" ([The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/are-shorts-worth-it)). The recommended architecture is a **hub plus a selective spoke**. Podcast research found full episodes about twice as popular as clips among committed viewers, with clips rarely substituting for them ([Sounds Profitable](https://soundsprofitable.com/article/12-07-article/)). That supports one strong hub with occasional derivatives.

| Product | Slot | Length | Role | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hub masterclass** | Sun 6:00 PM IST (as a Premiere; owner decision) | 28–38 min; 40-min overflow trigger | The weekly course | Every week |
| **Decode Extended** (standalone Deep Dive) | Wed 6:00 PM IST | 10–15 min | Search and evergreen depth; the Mains walk-through | 0–1 a week by default, 2 only in a heavy week with editor capacity |
| **Shorts** | Mon–Fri | 45–60 s | Reach, single trap or Glimpse, MCQ answer | 3–5 a week; one APPSC-tagged until 24 Jan 2027 |
| **Monthly compilation** | First Saturday of the next month (to avoid clashing with the Sunday hub; owner decision) | 60–90 min | Spaced second viewing | Rebuilt from existing renders only |

The Extended cut fills two gaps at once. It clears YouTube's **8-minute mid-roll threshold** ([YouTube Help 6175006](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6175006)), which a 5–8-minute re-cut would not. And it gives map-led conflict geography its own title and thumbnail. That format had the highest reach found in the earlier mentor research: Khan GS Research Centre's top video, about 24.6 million views, is a Russia–Ukraine map explainer ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@khangsresearchcentre1685/)). The hub version and the Extended cut share renders. The Extended cut adds the extension blocks plus a fresh 20–30-second open and close, sits in the story-arc playlist ("Russia–Ukraine arc") and gets a card from the matching hub chapter. Its title must take a different angle from the hub's. "Why a Truce in One Town Is Not Peace" against the hub's week-level title is the pattern.

**Production capacity** favours this plan over a full split. Audiobook practice puts recording at about **2 hours at the mic per finished hour** for professionals and about 3 for newcomers, with **no more than 3–4 hours of recording a day** ([Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-to-record-an-audiobook-guide-74974/); [Survival First](https://survivalfirst.substack.com/p/making-survival-first-part-8-recording)) **[industry anecdote, not voice science]**. A heavy week's new narration (a 40-minute hub, about 6 minutes of extension blocks and fresh open/close, and 4 minutes of Shorts) comes to about 50 finished minutes. At a 2–3x ratio that is **about 100–150 minutes at the mic**, which splits comfortably into two Saturday sessions of 60–75 minutes each. The voice is not the bottleneck. The editor's hours are. Practitioner estimates run **1–2 hours per finished minute with existing graphics, falling to 15–30 minutes once presets exist** ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/after-effects-discussions/how-long-would-it-take-to-create-animations-such-as-these/m-p/9901648)) **[anecdotal]**. At 0.5–1 hour per minute, 50 finished minutes is **25–50 editor-hours**. That is tolerable only if the News Grid, Trap Vault, Update Desk, Decode Desk and mind-map builds are **templated** in the repo renderer, and if the Extended cut reuses hub renders rather than rebuilding them. A full split (a hub plus three or four bespoke standalones) would roughly double the packaging work and break that budget.

## Runtime budgets for a typical and a heavy week

Word budgets use the 135-wpm rule (minutes × 135). Times include the 6–8-second checkpoint holds.

| Segment | Typical week (~32:45, ~4,420 words) | Heavy week (~40:00, ~5,400 words) |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open (pre-question + "On today's desk") | 1:15 | 1:30 |
| Update Desk | 2:00 (2–3 updates × 40 s) | 3:00 (4 updates; anything larger escalates to a Deep Dive) |
| Deep Dive 1 (lead) | 6:30 | 7:30 (Extended cut on Wed) |
| Deep Dive 2 | 6:00 | 7:00 |
| Deep Dive 3 | 5:30 | 6:30 |
| Deep Dive 4 | none | Not in the hub as a Deep Dive; it airs as a **2:30 Focus "trailer"** with a card to its Extended cut, or is held as next week's Update |
| Focus segments | 2 × 2:30 = 5:00 | 3 × 2:10 = 6:30 |
| Telugu States Lens | Inside its pillar (45–90 s, counted above) | Same |
| News Grid | 3:30 (about 7 Glimpses, scope order) | 4:00 (about 8–9 Glimpses; the rest go to the PDF "Also in the news" list) |
| Trap Vault (this week + 2 from last week + 1 from 4 weeks ago) | 1:45 | 2:00 |
| Desk Note (optional) | 0:00–0:45 | Dropped |
| Verdict + CTA (15–20 s over the Desk for the end screen) | 1:15 | 1:00 |
| **Total** | **≈32:45** | **≈40:00** (plus a 10–15 min Extended cut) |

A **light week** (two Deep Dives) should land at 22–26 minutes and must not be padded to reach the typical length. The Oleshky sample shows what the lead Deep Dive in a heavy week looks like after modularisation. The hub core runs about 7:30, and the Extended cut runs about 12–13 minutes: the full 1,571-word pillar plus a new 20–30-second open and close.

**Trimming rules when a week overflows.** The cascade runs in this order and stops as soon as the projected runtime falls to 38 minutes or less.

1. **Cap every Deep Dive at 8:00 in the hub.** Move extension blocks to the Extended cut or the PDF.
2. **Cap each Update at 45 s.** An Update that needs more than 3 minutes is a Deep Dive and competes for a pillar slot.
3. **Cap the News Grid at about 9 Glimpses.** Surplus Glimpses go to the PDF "Also in the news" list. Any that have a map or a single sharp trap become Short candidates.
4. **Demote by score.** The pillar with the lowest selection-test score (§3 item 3) drops from Deep Dive to Focus, and a single-fact Focus drops to a Glimpse.
5. **Route the fourth Deep Dive to Decode Extended.** It appears in the hub only as a Focus-length trailer. If editor capacity is short that week, it is held as next week's Update instead.
6. **Defer what has no deadline.** Mains-only, evergreen items with no Prelims or APPSC urgency are held for next week or the monthly compilation and logged in the README carry-forward list.
7. **Drop the Desk Note,** then shorten the verdict to 1:00.

Three things are never cut: the cold-open pre-question, one checkpoint per pillar, and the Trap Vault's spaced recall. The retrieval evidence above is the main reason a long video teaches at all. A genuine Telugu States Lens stays in the hub until 24 January 2027; if it must leave, it becomes the week's APPSC Short, not nothing. The owner can override the 40-minute trigger for an exceptional week (a Budget week, for example), provided the brief records why and every chapter stays at 8 minutes or less.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Hub length policy.** Approve 28–38 min (default about 32) with a 40-min overflow trigger, replacing "typically 20–35, no fixed cap" (decision 19). The cap stays soft: the owner can override it with a reason logged.
2. **Decode Extended.** Approve 0–1 standalone Deep Dives a week on Wednesday 6 PM, or reject the format and keep everything in the hub plus the PDF.
3. **Premiere.** Decide whether to run the Sunday hub as a YouTube Premiere (live chat and reminders).
4. **Monetisation priority.** If mid-roll revenue matters once the channel is in the YouTube Partner Programme, every Extended cut must run at least 8 min (the current YPP thresholds were not verified).
5. **Editor capacity.** State how many editor-hours a week are available. At an estimated 25–50 hours for a heavy week, that number decides whether an Extended cut ships in a given week.
6. **Monthly slot.** Move the monthly compilation to the first Saturday of the next month so it doesn't share a Sunday with the hub.
7. **Length experiment.** Approve an 8-week alternating-length test, with the results logged in the Monday reviews.

## Conclusion

The real choice was never "short video or long video". It is **where depth lives**. The Oleshky sample proves the channel can produce a 14-minute pillar worth watching. The research says a weekly course stays learnable at about 30–40 minutes only if it is broken into chapters of 8 minutes or less with retrieval between them. Writing every Deep Dive as a hub core plus detachable extension blocks resolves the tension without a second script or a second visual build. The hub stays a promise an aspirant can keep alongside a newspaper, and the best pillar each week gets a second life as a search-facing standalone.

The weakest link in this recommendation is evidence about this exact audience. No length, speed or retention data specific to UPSC or Group-1 aspirants surfaced, and every platform figure here is second-hand. The proposed ranges should therefore be treated as a **starting hypothesis that the channel's own chapter-level retention data will overrule** within two months. The rule that matters most is the one that makes that possible: chapter every pillar, and read the per-chapter curve every Monday.
