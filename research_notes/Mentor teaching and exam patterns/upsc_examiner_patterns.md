## 1. Prelims formats, the post-2022 shift, and verified example questions

### Takeaway
Since 2023 UPSC has deliberately killed option-elimination. It now relies on "How many of the above…" counting options ("Only one / Only two / All three / None"), Statement-I/Statement-II reasoning pairs, and "how many pairs are correctly matched". Current events are usually the *trigger*, such as a war, a country in the news or a mineral race, while the tested fact is static (a border, a capital, a resource or a constitutional status). That is exactly the "dynamic trigger → static anchor" structure of our 5-step format.

### Inferences
- The examiner uses the news as the *selection mechanism* (why this country, mineral or body today) but tests a *static, checkable fact* (border, resource, constitutional status, publisher). A script segment should show both halves: "Here is the headline. Here is the frozen fact UPSC will pin to it."
- Counting formats punish partial knowledge. A viewer must know the status of *every* item, so Trap Vault lines should cover the whole set of look-alikes (all of Ukraine's neighbours, all of the "National Commissions"), not just the correct one.
- "False headline" distractors (Turkey quitting NATO) show that the examiner tests whether the aspirant actually read the news or only skimmed headlines.

## 2. Share of current-affairs-linked questions in Prelims, 2019–2026

### Takeaway
There is no agreed number. "Direct" current-affairs counts run at about 13–24 questions out of 100 depending on the institute and the year. Analysts who count "current-affairs-linked" (static questions triggered by news) put it at around 40% or more. Scripts should say "a large share" rather than quote a single figure, or should attribute any figure to the institute that produced it.

### Cited Findings
- Unacademy table of "current affairs" counts: 2021 – 15, 2022 – 18, 2023 – 18, 2024 – 13, 2025 – 13. — [Unacademy Prelims paper analysis](https://unacademy.com/content/upsc/upsc-prelims-paper-analysis/)
- Vajiram's subject-weightage page puts current affairs at "22–24% (Direct + Indirect)", with 24 questions in 2023 and 13 (miscellaneous) in 2024. This **conflicts** with Unacademy's 18 for 2023. — [Vajiram subject-wise weightage](https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/upsc-prelims-subject-wise-weightage/)
- A Medium compilation says current affairs averaged 23.8 questions across 2021–2025, and that counting direct and indirect questions pushes the weightage to around 40%. This is a low-authority source. — [Medium compilation](https://medium.com/@airo_87400/upsc-prelims-subject-wise-weightage-2021-2025-8bbd24798f62)
- Bharat Notes, drawing on Vision/Drishti/ForumIAS analyses, puts 2020 at about 60% static and 40% dynamic. — [Bharat Notes FAQ](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/prelims/static-vs-dynamic-split-2020-2024/)
- For 2025 alone, the counts vary by institute: IASgyan 13 current affairs; PW OnlyIAS 23 "Current Affairs & Miscellaneous". Several analysts say UPSC "moved away from direct current affairs to embedding news topics into conceptual questions". — [IASgyan](https://www.iasgyan.in/blogs/detailed-analysis-of-upsc-paper-1-gs-prelims-2025); [PW OnlyIAS](https://pwonlyias.com/prelims-exam-analysis-2025/); [Aptiplus 2025](https://aptiplus.in/blogs/upsc-prelims-2025-analysis-cutoff-difficulty-subject-wise/)
- 2026 is described as "current affairs heavy" by InsightsIAS. No numeric share was retrievable. — [InsightsIAS](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/05/26/upsc-prelims-2026-analysis-a-lengthy-tough-and-current-affairs-heavy-paper/)

### Inferences
- The variance is definitional. A question on Ukraine's borders is "geography" to one institute and "current affairs" to another. For our channel, the useful framing is that pure current affairs is a minority of questions, while news-triggered static questions are a large and growing share.

### Gaps
- No consistent, single-methodology year-wise series for 2019–2026 was found. Counts for 2019 in particular were not retrievable.

## 3. Mains: how GS papers link current events to static content, directive words, and example questions

### Takeaway
Mains questions name the current event explicitly (Chandrayaan-3, NATO expansion, the India Semiconductor Mission, West Asian energy security). They then demand static scaffolding: the scheme's features, the treaty or institution, the geographic mechanism. The directive word sets the depth required. GS-1 geography questions are often triggered by global disasters in the news (for example tornadoes and cloudbursts).

**Directive words (conventional coaching interpretations; UPSC publishes no official glossary)**
- *Discuss*: a balanced presentation of several perspectives, then a reasoned conclusion.
- *Critically examine*: evaluation with judgment, covering validity, strengths and weaknesses, then a verdict.
- *Analyse*: break the issue into parts and examine the causes, effects and interlinkages.
- *Comment*: your interpretation, backed by evidence.
- *Evaluate / critically evaluate*: weigh merits and limitations, then deliver a judgment.
- *Describe*: list the features objectively, without personal judgment.
- One guide ranks *analyse/comment* as mid-demand and *evaluate/critically examine* as highest demand. Sources differ on the finer boundaries.
- Sources: [PMF IAS directive words](https://www.pmfias.com/directive-words/); [InsightsIAS directives explained](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2014/07/04/directives-explained-examine-critically-examine-analyse-critically-analyse/); [UPSCprep](https://www.upscprep.com/upsc-mains-keywords/)

### Inferences
- Multi-part Mains questions such as Chandrayaan-3 ("what / list / introduce / explain") reward factual recall of the news itself, such as the subsystems, the countries and the institution. A masterclass pillar should therefore include a "facts you must carry into Mains" block, not only analysis.
- IR questions are usually framed as a *contestable proposition* ("works well for India", "is transforming itself"). That supports a "Mains Lens" segment that states the proposition and gives both sides.

### Gaps
- Exact wording for most 2024–2026 GS questions could not be retrieved. Pull the official PDFs (see §6) before quoting any of them on screen.

## 4. Typical traps the examiner uses

### Takeaway
The recurring trap families appear in the verified questions above. They are: status confusion (constitutional vs statutory), publisher confusion, look-alike geography (near-neighbours, places with similar names), plausible false headlines, and over-reaching statements (superlatives such as "second largest" and "first-ever").

### Cited Findings
- **Constitutional vs statutory:** the 2023 "how many constitutional bodies" question (NCBC vs NHRC / Law Commission / NCDRC). Only NCBC is constitutional. — [Civilsdaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/prelims/consider-the-following-organizations-bodies-in-india/)
- **Publisher of index/report:** 2019, Global Competitiveness Report (WEF, not IMF/World Bank/UNCTAD). — [PMF IAS](https://www.pmfias.com/global-competitiveness-report-publisher-pyq-2019/)
- **Near-neighbour geography:** 2023 Ukraine (Czech Republic, Latvia and Lithuania are close but not bordering) and 2022 Afghanistan (Kyrgyzstan and Azerbaijan are close but not bordering). — [PMF IAS Ukraine](https://www.pmfias.com/which-countries-border-ukraine-pyq-2023/); [PMF IAS Afghanistan](https://www.pmfias.com/countries-bordering-afghanistan-pyq-2022/)
- **Misplaced region:** 2022 Cabo Delgado assigned to Spain (it is in Mozambique) and Catalonia assigned to Italy (it is in Spain). — [PMF IAS](https://www.pmfias.com/anatolia-amhara-cabo-delgado-catalonia-pyq-2022/)
- **False headline:** 2024 "Turkey rescinded NATO membership". — [Vajiram](https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/consider-the-following-pairs-37/)
- **Superlative/rank over-reach:** 2023 "Switzerland has the second largest gold reserves". — [PMF IAS](https://www.pmfias.com/switzerland-gold-exports-reserves-pyq-2023/)
- **Statement-II "explains" trap:** 2026 LT-LEDS/BUR-4. The dispute among answer keys shows how the "correct explanation" leg is itself the trap. — [ClearIAS 2026 key](https://www.clearias.com/upsc-prelims-2026-answer-key/)

### Inferences
- Ratification status, nodal-ministry and "first-ever" traps are standard in coaching literature and are part of our CLAUDE.md Trap Vault list, but I found **no verified 2019–2026 PYQ in this session** illustrating them. Do not claim a specific year for those traps without checking.
- Extreme words ("only", "all", "always") matter less in the post-2023 counting format, because the option no longer quotes a statement. The examiner instead embeds the over-reach inside a statement ("second largest", "rescinded").

## 6. Where to get authentic PYQs

### Takeaway
Use UPSC's own archive as the only on-screen source of question text. Coaching sites are useful for answer explanations but disagree on wording, numbering and even year, as with the Quad question that was really 2020.

### Cited Findings
- The official archive is at upsc.gov.in › Examinations › Previous Question Papers › Archives (https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers/archives). It has exam-name search and a year filter. The current-year papers sit at upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers. — [UPSC archive](https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers/archives)
- UPSC releases the Prelims official answer key only after the final result of that CSE cycle, so the 2026 Prelims key is still provisional (coaching keys only) as of Oct 2026. This is inferred from the 2026 search results, which showed only unofficial keys. — [ClearIAS 2026 key](https://www.clearias.com/upsc-prelims-2026-answer-key/)
- Reliable explainer sites that host question-by-question PYQ pages: PMF IAS (`pmfias.com/<topic>-pyq-<year>/`), Vajiram & Ravi, SuperKalam, Drishti (Mains solved papers by year), InsightsIAS (Mains papers posted on exam day).

### Inferences
- Production rule: any "How the examiner asked this" card should cite "UPSC CSE Prelims 2023, GS Paper I" and be checked against the official PDF, because third-party sites sometimes mislabel the year or paper. For example, one site tags the twister question as CSAT.

### Gaps
- I could not open upsc.gov.in directly (fetch was blocked) to confirm the current page layout or which years are hosted.
