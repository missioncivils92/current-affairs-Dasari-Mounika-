A 20–35 min weekly masterclass sits in a relatively uncrowded middle band. The large institutes appear to treat "weekly" as a secondary format.
- Vision IAS's monthly revision lengthened from 3–4 h to 5–6 h. If the two pages reflect a real change, coaching firms are expanding monthly compilations, not trimming them. That supports keeping our monthly compilation at 60–90 min as a *differentiated*, tighter product.

## 2. Do longer weekly compilations (45–90 min) get fewer or more views than shorter ones (15–30 min) in the UPSC niche?

### Takeaway
I found no study or dataset comparing views by length in the UPSC niche. The only available evidence is generic cross-niche benchmarks, in which retention percentage falls as length rises while total watch minutes can rise.

### Cited Findings
- No UPSC-specific length-vs-views data surfaced in searches of Reddit, Careers360, listicles or channel pages (see searches above; results contained no durations) — [Careers360 Q&A](https://www.careers360.com/question-will-going-through-only-youtube-the-hindu-daily-news-video-lecture-be-sufficient-for-upsc-current-affairs-portion/amp); [minitool listicle](https://youtubedownload.minitool.com/news/best-youtube-channel-for-current-affairs.html)
- Generic guide (not primary data): retention about 50–70% for videos under 5 min, 40–55% for 5–10 min, 35–45% for 10–20 min, 25–35% for 20+ min — [TubeLab](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention)
- A percentage comparison penalises long videos: four minutes watched is 40% of a 10-min video but 13% of a 30-min video — [Humble & Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/youtube-audience-retention-benchmarks)
- One blog claims educational videos peak at about 20–22 min, but cites no data — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/what-actually-makes-a-youtube-video-go-viral-in-2025)

### Inferences
- For a new channel, a 20–35 min weekly video should expect roughly 25–35% average percentage viewed under the generic bands, i.e., about 6–10 min average view duration. That is unverified and generic; replace it with our own YouTube Studio data after 4–6 uploads.
- A 45–90 min weekly video would likely post a lower percentage but potentially more absolute minutes per viewer. Since YouTube says satisfaction, not length, drives recommendation (Q3), the deciding factor is whether each added minute keeps people watching.

### Gaps
- No niche-specific A/B data. Our own analytics (retention curve, AVD by chapter) is the only reliable source. Suggested test: alternate ~22 min and ~35 min weeks for 6–8 weeks and compare impressions CTR, AVD, returning viewers and subs per 1,000 views.

## 3. YouTube retention benchmarks, chapters, and how YouTube treats video length

### Takeaway
The most-cited 2025 dataset (Retention Rabbit) puts average retention at about 23.7%, with educational how-tos among the best at about 42.1%. YouTube's own spokespeople say length is not a ranking factor in itself: make it "as long as it needs to be", optimise viewer value and satisfaction, and don't pad for watch time. Evidence that chapters improve retention is vendor-grade and mixed.

### Cited Findings
- **Retention Rabbit 2025 report** (via search summary; page could not be fetched): about 10,000+ videos and 150M+ minutes analysed; overall average retention 23.7%; 55% of viewers lost by the 60-second mark; only 16.8% of videos keep more than half their audience; educational how-tos average 42.1% retention (a top niche) — [Retention Rabbit report (Google-translate proxy link)](https://translate.google.com/translate?u=https://www.retentionrabbit.com/blog/2025-youtube-audience-retention-benchmark-report&hl=es&sl=en&tl=es&client=srp); figures repeated by [TubeLab](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention) and [Lenostube](https://www.lenostube.com/en/youtube-audience-retention-average-good-and-best-benchmarks/)
- A Stripo page attributes a length table to the report, but its numbers conflict with other sources, so it is not used — [Stripo research](https://research.stripo.email/youtube-benchmarks)
- **YouTube (Todd Beaupré, Senior Director, Growth & Discovery)**, as quoted in a vidIQ summary: "stop worrying about watch time and stretching out those videos as long as possible and instead focus on maximizing the value that you're delivering to your viewers"; watch time is one signal among many, alongside satisfaction — [vidIQ summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/youtube-launches-new-algorithm-updates-2025). "Make the video as long as it needs to be" is also attributed to Beaupré — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/mrbeast-explains-youtubes-algorithm-205044292.html). (Secondhand; the exact phrase "no ideal length" was **not** found.)
- **Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison)**: a video should be "as long as it needs to be, but no longer"; recommendations are tied to viewer satisfaction, not length (secondhand quotes; the source mixes Shorts context) — [ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-long-can-a-youtube-video-be-in-2026-best-length-guide/); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length); his role since Jul 2022 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rene_Ritchie)
- **Chapters:** they can *lower* average view duration because viewers jump around, and Key Moments in Search can drop viewers mid-video; they tend to help engagement in educational and how-to content — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-chapters-key-moments/). A Munch 2024 analysis (1,000+ videos) found 2.18–2.8× higher like-to-view ratios with chapters (engagement, not retention). A "Wistia 2021: up to 20% higher completion" claim circulates without a primary link — [gyre.pro](https://gyre.pro/blog/youtube-video-chapters-how-to-add-them-why-they-increase-views); [timeskip.io](https://timeskip.io/blog/how-chapter-markers-can-boost-viewer-retention) (vendor claims, unverified)

### Inferences
- The 55% drop by 60 seconds supports CLAUDE.md's cold-open rule: start inside the event, put the pre-question before the 1:00 mark, and avoid "Welcome back".
- Chapters fit our "every minute must earn its place" stance. They may cost some raw AVD, but they serve returning revision viewers and search entry, which suits a reference-style weekly masterclass. Judge them by returning viewers and session time, not AVD alone.
- YouTube's guidance supports CLAUDE.md §2's "sized to the week's density, no fixed cap" policy rather than a hard target.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube page with length-band retention benchmarks; YouTube does not publish them. Retention Rabbit's length breakdown could not be read directly. No rigorous, peer-reviewed study of chapters and retention.

## 4. Evidence on 1.5x/2x playback among Indian students and aspirants

### Takeaway
I found no India- or UPSC-specific survey of playback-speed usage. Western studies show most students speed up lectures and that comprehension holds up to about 2x, then falls off. Dense material may suffer more.

### Cited Findings
- UCLA lab study (Murphy et al., 2021/22): minimal comprehension cost from 1x to 1.5x or 2x, with a decline beyond 2x; watching twice at 2x can help — [UCLA paper PDF](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf); [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/watching-lecture-twice-double-speed-can-benefit-learning)
- Reported survey (in coverage of the UCLA work): 85% of students usually watched lectures faster than normal, 23% at double speed — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/no-major-harm-learning-double-speed-lecture-viewing) (via search summary)
- Engineering-course study: about 55% of students chose 1.5x/2x, 45% normal or live; grades were similar — [Mohammadpanah & Atabaki, Infonomics Society](https://infonomics-society.org/wp-content/uploads/Asynchronous-Lectures-View-Speed-Effect.pdf)
- Medical students: no significant difference in concentration or long-term retention across playback speeds — [BMC Medical Education 2023 (PMC10354881)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10354881/)
- Caveat from BPS: the test material was simple (real-estate appraisal, the Roman Empire), so complex content may behave differently — [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/watching-lecture-twice-double-speed-can-benefit-learning)

### Inferences
- Assume a large share of viewers watch at 1.25–2x. At 1.5x, a 30-min masterclass costs about 20 min. Our 130–140 wpm narration with slower trap and number rows (110–120 wpm) suits speed-watching. On-screen keyword cards and the "Pause and answer" 5-second countdown help speed-watchers catch the key facts. A 5-second hold at 2x is only 2.5 s, so consider 6–8 s cards.
- Playback speed makes runtime matter less to motivated aspirants than to casual viewers. This weakly supports a density-sized 25–35 min runtime over a forced 15-min cap.

### Gaps
- No Indian edtech (Unacademy/PW/StudyIQ app) or UPSC aspirant survey on playback speed was found. YouTube Studio does not expose playback-speed data to creators, so this stays a gap for us too.
